# SPL Protein Hack System

_A simple, repeatable system for hitting high-protein targets in modern life, built around two engineered drinks per day._

---

> **TLDR****Daily band:** aiming near **2.0 g/kg/day** for trained, often older adults.**Per-hit band:** targeting **~0.4–0.5 g/kg** engineered events where possible, with **~20–30 g** liquid anchors as the practical implementation.**Mechanism:** two low-friction Anchor Drinks per day as the smallest change that reliably pushes people into the evidence-supported protein ranges, while leaving the rest of their life mostly intact.

# 1. OVERVIEW

In SPL, protein is **infrastructure**, not a “gym macro”.

It is the raw material for:

- Muscle and strength  
- Tendons, ligaments, cartilage  
- Gut lining and immune proteins  
- Enzymes, hormones  
- Skin, hair, nails

From the 30s and 40s onward:

- Muscle becomes **less responsive** to each protein dose (anabolic resistance). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolotherapy?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
- Repair demand across the body **increases** (more mileage, low-grade inflammation).

The SPL stance:

> Keep protein **high**, and make the behaviour change **as small, cheap, and repeatable as possible**.

Rather than rebuilding someone’s entire diet, SPL focuses on a single, high-leverage shift:

> **Replace two existing “drink moments” (coffee, latte, frappe, bubble tea, etc.) with two SPL Anchor Drinks.**

Done consistently, this quietly adds **~40–60 g of protein per day** with almost no disruption to social life, cooking ability, or schedule.

# 2. PROTEIN DOSING IN SPL — HOW MUCH & WHY

## 2.1 Daily targets

For adults running SPL protocols (Gateway + Canon), we think in three ranges:

- **RDA / survival band**\
~**0.8 g/kg/day**\
– Classic RDA; enough to prevent deficiency, often low for strength and ageing.  
- **Evidence band for ageing + function**\
**1.0–1.3 g/kg/day**\
– Multiple expert groups recommend this for older adults (~65+) to maintain muscle and function, with higher intakes for those who are active and/or ill. ([orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org](https://orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org/article/129086-the-effects-of-type-i-collagen-hydrolysate-supplementation-on-bones-muscles-and-joints-a-systematic-review?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
- **SPL performance & redundancy band**\
**~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**\
– Meta-analyses of resistance training + protein show that gains in fat-free mass plateau around **~1.6 g/kg/day**, with **2.2 g/kg/day** often treated as a reasonable upper bound in the literature. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1646090/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

SPL places most trainees around **~2.0 g/kg/day**, because:

- They are **training regularly**, not sedentary.  
- Many are **30+ / 40+**, and we want to over-deliver building blocks, not under-deliver.  
- Extra protein supports not only muscle but also **connective tissue, immune function, and general repair**.

For a **75 kg** person, that’s:

> **~150 g protein per day** as a working target in the Canon.

Two Anchor Drinks contribute **~40–60 g** of this with minimal friction; the rest comes from regular meals.

---

## 2.2 Per-hit dosing & age

Muscle has a “volume knob” for each protein dose:

- A single meal of **~0.3–0.4 g/kg** high-quality protein is enough to **max out muscle protein synthesis (MPS)** for many adults. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-17505-0?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
- With ageing, **anabolic resistance** raises that per-meal threshold – doses closer to **0.4–0.5 g/kg** seem more reliable to get the same effect in older adults. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolotherapy?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

SPL therefore normalises:

> **~0.4–0.5 g/kg per deliberately engineered protein “event”**\
> (operationally, **~25–40 g** protein per drink for most adults).

We don’t try to micromanage every gram. Instead:

- Each Anchor Drink delivers **~20–30 g** protein.  
- For lighter people that is already in the “MPS-maxing” zone.  
- For heavier people, **two drinks plus normal meals** reliably move them into the 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day band across the whole day.

The system is built on **repeatable, low-friction units**, not precise lab dosing.

# 3. HOW SPL EVALUATES PROTEIN SOURCES (THE MATRIX)

When SPL recommends a protein source, we care about more than the label number.

We use four axes:

1. **Dose density**\
– How much “mouth-work” and volume do you need for ~25–30 g protein?  
1. **Prep time**\
– How much time / equipment / effort to prepare that dose?  
1. **Bloat / ease of digestion**\
– How likely you are to feel heavy, gassy, or sluggish afterwards.  
1. **Shelf life**\
– Can it live in a cupboard, drawer, or suitcase without drama?

These axes explain why liquids are often the best **behavioural starting point**, even though whole foods matter.

## 3.1 Example matrix for ~25–30 g protein

_(Illustrative examples to define the axes; Anchor Plates are treated elsewhere.)_

|Source (\~25–30 g protein)|Prep Time|Bloat / Gut Load|Fill (how “big” it feels)|Shelf Life|
|--|--|--|--|--|
|**SPL Anchor Drink** (whey/plant in water/soy)|**Low**|**Low–Medium** (usually light)|**Low** (small glass)|**Long** (dry powders)|
|**Canned tuna** (≈ 1 can)|Low|Medium|Medium|Long|
|**Eggs** (4–5 eggs)|Medium|Medium–High|High|Medium|
|**Steak** (~120–150 g cooked)|High|Medium–High|High|Short|

From this matrix:

- The **Anchor Drink** sits in the corner we care about most for daily adherence:\
**low prep, low fill, long shelf life**, acceptable gut load.  
- Foods like tuna, eggs, steak are valuable but higher friction; they live in the **“meal” and “anchor plate”** space, not in the **first behavioural hack**.

For the initial protein intervention, SPL prioritises the **lowest-friction corner**: engineered drinks.

# 4. SPL ANCHOR DRINK SYSTEM

## 4.1 Design principles

An SPL Anchor Drink must:

- Deliver **~20–30 g protein** per serving.  
- Work with **just powder + water + something to stir**.  
- Use **shelf-stable raw materials** (unflavoured where possible).  
- Require **no blender** and minimal equipment.  
- Be easily **customisable** (you control flavour and sweetness).  
- Realistically **replace existing caffeinated or sweet drink rituals**.

Core behavioural question:

> **What is the smallest daily change that meaningfully improves protein intake?**

Answer: **two Anchor Drinks per day, inside slots you already use for coffee/tea/etc.**

---

## 4.2 Protein core variants

Every Anchor Drink starts with:

> **1 scoop of protein powder ≈ 20–25 g protein.**

That scoop can be:

1. **Whey protein (concentrate or isolate) (unflavoured preferred)**

    - Complete amino acid profile; high leucine.  
    - Consistently shown to support muscle mass and strength when combined with resistance training. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1646090/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Collagen peptides (unflavoured)**

    - Rich in glycine, proline, hydroxyproline.  
    - Not a complete protein, so it **supplements**, not replaces, other protein sources.  
    - Emerging evidence suggests benefits for joint pain, function, and connective tissue when used over months, especially in osteoarthritis and active adults. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11842160/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Soy isolate / other plant isolates or blends (unflavoured)**

    - Soy, pea + rice, etc.  
    - Useful for those avoiding dairy or animal protein; when properly formulated, these can support muscle gains similarly to animal protein in many contexts. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollagen-Hydrolysat?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

**Canon stance on flavour:**

- **Unflavoured powders** are preferred as the default.  
- Flavoured products are _allowed_ but not canonical, because they often add:  
    - artificial sweeteners,  
    - sugar alcohols,  
    - gums,  
    - “natural flavour” blends.

> In SPL, flavour is something **you add on purpose**, not something buried in a proprietary formula.

---

## 4.3 Liquid and optional creaminess layer

The protein scoop needs a carrier. SPL standard options:

1. **Water (minimum viable drink)**

    - Works in any environment (office, travel, hotel).  
    - Lowest volume and complexity.  
    - Taste is functional, not luxurious, but completely valid.  
    - Works well with clear isolates to give a juice/sports drink like feel.

1. **Soy milk / soy milk powder + water** _(SPL default)_

    - Powdered soy milk is **shelf-stable** and easy to store.  
    - Often gentler on digestion than combining whey with dairy milk for many people.  
    - Adds extra protein and creaminess.

1. **Dairy milk**

    - Good option for those who tolerate lactose and dairy fats well.  
    - Can increase bloat in some; treated as an **optional upgrade**, not default.

Design rule:

> **Every Anchor Drink must remain functional as “protein powder + water”.**\
> Other liquids exist to improve experience, not to make the system fragile.

---

## 4.4 Flavour and sweetness — SPL position

The core drink is **already complete for protein** if it contains:

> **1 scoop protein + a liquid.**

Flavour makes it feel like a genuine replacement for lattes, frappes, or bubble tea.

### Flavour inputs

SPL uses **single-ingredient, understandable flavour tools**:

- **Coffee / caffeine**  
    - Freeze-dried instant coffee  
    - Espresso shot  
    - Strong brewed coffee  
- **Tea / green profile**  
    - Matcha powder  
    - Other pure tea powders  
- **Greens / health layer**  
    - Chlorella, spirulina  
    - Greens mixes (e.g. AG1-type products) if already in use  
    - Evidence for broad health benefits of greens powders is mixed, but they can be a convenient way to add phytonutrients.  
- **Other flavour**  
    - Cocoa powder  
    - Spices like cinnamon or nutmeg

### Sweetness options

SPL baseline:

- Try **no added sweetener** first, or **less** than your current drinks.  
- If sweetness is needed:  
    - **Honey**  
    - **Date syrup**  
    - **Maple syrup**

These are still **sugars** and will raise blood glucose, but they can be easier to dose and may offer minor differences in glycaemic response and extra micronutrients compared to refined table sugar. The main win is usually **total sugar reduction**, not magic properties. ([SpringerLink](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02079-0?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

#### [Artificial Sweeteners (NSS)  (Details in Appendix 1)](https://slite.com/api/public/notes/8pez6QXEbXZzZT/redirect)

Artificial sweeteners (stevia, sucralose, etc.):

- **Not banned** in SPL, and they are effective for replacing high-sugar drinks initially.  
- They are acceptable as a **transitional tool** if that’s the only way someone will consistently replace a high-sugar drink with an Anchor Drink.  
- **Critical SPL Caveat:** The canon preference is to minimize reliance on artificial sweeteners over the long term. Recent systematic reviews and WHO guidance have linked chronic high intake of Non-Sugar Sweeteners (NSS) to a potential **increased risk of cardiovascular diseases, stroke, and all-cause mortality**.  
    - **SPL Rule for Long-Term Use:** Do not view NSS as a permanent, risk-free substitute for sugar. The long-term ideal is always to move the Anchor Drink to the **unflavoured/unsweetened baseline**.

Core rule:

> **The Anchor Drink is “one scoop of raw protein + a simple liquid”.**\
> **Flavour and sweetness are optional, conscious layers.**

# 5. THE TWO-DRINK HACK

## 5.1 Structure in daily life

The central SPL move:

> **Have two Anchor Drinks per day, in place of two existing drink rituals.**

For a **70–80 kg** adult, a typical pattern:

- **Anchor Drink 1 — Morning**  
    - 1 scoop **whey** + water/soy (+ optional coffee/matcha/greens).  
    - ≈ **20–30 g protein**.  
    - Functions as the **first “meal”** even with low morning appetite.  
- **Anchor Drink 2 — Later in the day**  
    - 1 scoop **collagen** _or_ plant isolate + water/soy/milk.  
    - ≈ **20–30 g protein**.  
    - Adds **total daily protein** and connective-tissue bias with low gut load.

Total from drinks:

> **~40–60 g protein per day** with 3–4 minutes of total work.

When added to normal eating, this commonly moves users from **sub-1 g/kg/day** into the **1.4–2.0+ g/kg/day** zone – exactly where multiple lines of evidence suggest better muscle maintenance, strength and function, especially in trained or older adults. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1646090/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

## 5.2 Behaviour framing

We are _not_ trying to create a “shake lifestyle”.

Internal SPL framing:

> “You already have drink rituals.\
> We are just changing **what’s in the cup**, not whether you hold a cup.”

Examples of swaps:

- Iced latte → whey + instant coffee + soy.  
- Bubble tea → plant isolate + matcha + soy.  
- Sweet evening drink → collagen + cocoa + soy.

The person keeps the **ritual**, swaps the **payload**.

# 6. SPL PROTEIN DRINK CHEAT SHEET

- **Daily target for SPL trainees:**\
Aim around **2.0 g/kg/day** protein (higher than RDA but within studied safe ranges for healthy adults). ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1646090/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
- **Per-event target:**\
**~0.4–0.5 g/kg** to robustly stimulate MPS, especially in older or trained adults. In practice for drinks, we use **~20–30 g** protein per serving and let total daily intake do the rest. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-17505-0?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
- **Anchor Drink template:**  
    - 1 scoop **unflavoured** protein (whey / collagen / soy / other plant isolate), ≈ **20–25 g**.  
        - Water, or soy milk / soy powder + water, or milk.  
        - Optional coffee/tea/greens and minimal sweetening if required.  
- **Two Anchor Drinks per day:**  
    - Add **~40–60 g protein**.  
    - Use **shelf-stable powders** and **no blender**.  
    - Implemented by **replacing** existing drink habits, not stacking extra chores.  
- **Matrix position:**\
Anchor Drinks live at:  
    - **Low prep time**  
    - **Low gut load**  
    - **Low fill per 25–30 g protein**  
    - **Long shelf life**

This makes them the **first-line intervention** in SPL’s nutrition layer.

- **Safety note:**\
High-protein intakes in the SPL range appear safe for **healthy kidneys** in current literature, but people with **chronic kidney disease or other renal issues** must work with their medical team, as standard care often includes lower-protein prescriptions. ([arthritis.org](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/treatment/complementary-therapies/supplements-and-vitamins/can-collagen-supplements-help-arthritis?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

# 7. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION & REFERENCES

This section summarises the key scientific pillars behind the Anchor Drink system.

1. **Higher protein needs in older adults:**

    - The PROT-AGE Study Group and other reviews conclude older adults generally benefit from **1.0–1.3 g/kg/day** to maintain function, above the classic 0.8 g/kg/day RDA, with higher intakes (≥1.2 g/kg/day) for active or ill individuals. ([orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org](https://orthopedicreviews.openmedicalpublishing.org/article/129086-the-effects-of-type-i-collagen-hydrolysate-supplementation-on-bones-muscles-and-joints-a-systematic-review?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Optimal daily intake for trained adults:**

    - Meta-analyses of resistance training + protein supplementation suggest **~1.6 g/kg/day** maximises gains in fat-free mass, with little extra hypertrophy beyond that point, and **2.2 g/kg/day** frequently cited as an upper boundary in research. ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1646090/full?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
    - More recent work indicates benefits for strength and lean mass in older and younger adults at **~1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, with somewhat higher intakes helpful for some trainees. ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollagen-Hydrolysat?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Per-meal dosing and anabolic resistance:**

    - Schoenfeld & Aragon (2018) and others propose that **~0.4 g/kg/meal** across at least four meals (~1.6 g/kg/day) effectively maximizes anabolism, with up to **0.55 g/kg/meal** required at the upper end (2.2 g/kg/day). ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-17505-0?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )  
    - Guidance for master athletes and older adults similarly frames **0.3–0.4 g/kg/meal**, up to **0.5 g/kg** around exercise, as sufficient to stimulate muscle protein synthesis, consistent with the SPL “0.4–0.5 g/kg” per hit heuristic. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelet-rich_plasma?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **High-protein safety in healthy individuals:**

    - Reviews on kidney health and protein intake suggest that in **healthy adults**, intakes up to **~2.0 g/kg/day** are generally safe, with evidence of tolerable upper limits even higher in athletes; in contrast, lower protein is often recommended in established chronic kidney disease. ([arthritis.org](https://www.arthritis.org/health-wellness/treatment/complementary-therapies/supplements-and-vitamins/can-collagen-supplements-help-arthritis?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Collagen as a connective-tissue adjunct:**

    - Systematic reviews and RCTs indicate collagen peptide supplementation can reduce joint pain and improve function in osteoarthritis and physically active adults, with a generally good safety profile, although some trials show mixed or placebo-equivalent results and further research is needed. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11842160/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

1. **Distribution and timing:**

    - Work on protein timing supports the idea that **spreading protein across the day** (e.g. 3–4 reasonably sized doses) is superior to highly skewed patterns for muscle accretion, aligning with SPL’s “multiple hits” architecture. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-17505-0?utm_source=chatgpt.com) )

# Appendix 1 - Sweetener Nuance — The Transitional Tool

SPL’s stance on flavour and sweetness is deliberately boring:

> **Use sweeteners only as a short-term bridge.**\
> **Long-term target = less sweetness overall, not “better” sweeteners.**

Originally we framed artificial / non-sugar sweeteners (NSS) as something to minimise because of “ongoing debate” around appetite, microbiome, and cardiometabolic risk. That’s still true, but the **weight of concern has shifted**: the cardiometabolic signal is now the bigger story.

### 1. What newer data are actually saying

Several large prospective cohorts and umbrella/meta-analyses have now linked **higher intake of NSS or artificially sweetened beverages** with:

- Increased risk of **cardiovascular disease (CVD) and stroke**  
    - e.g. the NutriNet-Santé cohort found higher intake of total artificial sweeteners was associated with increased risk of overall CVD and cerebrovascular disease, with specific signals for aspartame (stroke) and sucralose/acesulfame-K (coronary disease).  
- Increased risk of **all-cause and CVD mortality, hypertension, and type 2 diabetes**, even after adjusting for confounders, though often with smaller effect sizes than sugar-sweetened beverages (SSBs).

These are observational data (so residual confounding is always an issue), but the consistency across multiple cohorts and meta-analyses means we **can’t treat NSS as neutral** anymore, especially when exposure is daily and long-term.

On top of that, in 2023 the **World Health Organization** issued a guideline that:

> _“suggests that non-sugar sweeteners (NSS) not be used as a means of achieving weight control or reducing the risk of noncommunicable diseases”_ — a **conditional recommendation** based on evidence showing:

- _no meaningful long-term benefit for fat loss_, and  
- possible undesirable effects on cardiometabolic outcomes.

So: the debate is still technically “ongoing”, but the direction of travel is clear — **less enthusiasm, more caution.**

---

### 2. How SPL updates its stance

SPL keeps the **transitional tool** concept but tightens the language:

1. **Primary target is “less sweetness”, not “safer sweetness”.**

    - WHO explicitly recommends reducing the _overall sweetness_ of the diet, regardless of whether it comes from sugar or NSS.  
    - SPL aligns with this: the palate should shift over time towards **less sweet drinks and foods in general**.

1. **NSS as a bridge, not a lifestyle.**

    - Best-case, artificial sweeteners are used to **replace high-sugar beverages** (SSBs) in the short term, reducing free-sugar load while people untangle their behaviour.  
    - They are **not** something SPL wants people to rely on for years as a background constant.

1. **Cardiometabolic risk is now the main worry.**

    - Previously we emphasised microbiome and appetite loops.  
    - Updated Canon: the **stronger concern** is the **association with CVD, stroke, and mortality**, especially at high, chronic intakes.

1. **Risk hierarchy stays intact:**

    - **High-sugar drinks daily** → clearly bad (weight, diabetes, CVD).  
    - **NSS drinks daily** → probably _less bad than sugar_ but **not neutral**, and not a health upgrade.  
    - **Unsweetened / minimally sweet drinks** → the actual goal.

## 6. SPL SUMMARY

When you put **cost** and **friction** together:

- **Cheapest raw protein:** frozen **chicken breast**.  
- **Cheapest “just add water” proteins:** **soy isolate**, then **whey**.  
- **Most behaviourally efficient SPL move:**  
    - **Two anchor drinks per day** (whey/soy/collagen + Unisoy),  
    - Total cost **under S$4/day** in Singapore,  
    - For ~40–45 g protein with **~2 minutes of work**.

Everything else (eggs, chicken plates, steak, tuna) then sits on top as **flavour, culture, and satiety** rather than the core protein “hack”.
