# I. NUTRITION & METABOLISM

## 1. Protein is non-negotiable after 40

Protein is structural — muscle, bone matrix, immune cells, enzymes, hormones, neurotransmitters — it’s everywhere.

As you age, anabolic resistance sets in. Your body becomes less efficient at using dietary protein to maintain and build muscle.

Most standard guidelines (0.8 g/kg) prevent deficiency, not decline.

Evidence consistently shows that 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day is a strong baseline for adults over 40, and going toward 1.6–2.0+ g/kg is beneficial for active adults, strength-training adults, or people trying to preserve or regain muscle.

Protein is not your “primary energy source” — carbs and fats still run that show — but protein is the raw material that keeps you from sliding into sarcopenia, weak bones, metabolic instability, and the slow erosion of independence.

Carbs and fats flex depending on lifestyle.\
Protein does not.

**Protein is the adult macronutrient.**

## 2. Dietary cholesterol ≠ blood cholesterol

The old belief that eating cholesterol raises your blood cholesterol came from early observational data that didn’t hold up under modern study designs.

Your liver maintains cholesterol balance through internal regulation:\
eat more → it produces less;\
eat less → it produces more.

Eggs and dietary cholesterol don’t meaningfully affect LDL particle number (ApoB) — the actual marker that drives plaque formation and cardiovascular risk.

The real risk variable isn’t the cholesterol in your food.\
It’s the number of lipoprotein particles in your bloodstream.

**Fear outdated models, not eggs.**

Use modern markers, not 1970s nutrition pamphlets.

## 3. Sugar isn’t the enemy — glycemic chaos is

Your body doesn’t implode because you ate a banana.

It struggles when refined, ultra-processed sugars hit your bloodstream in massive, fast spikes.

The problem isn’t “sweetness” — it’s rate of absorption and metabolic volatility.

Refined sugars → rapid glucose spikes → rapid crashes → hunger → cravings → unstable energy → metabolic strain.

Meanwhile, fruit, dates, honey, and minimally processed carbs tend to come with fiber and micronutrients that slow the absorption curve.

Artificial sweeteners are safe in toxicology, but their behavioral impact varies wildly across individuals — appetite, cravings, gut effects — making them more psychology than physiology for many people.

**SPL avoids glycemic chaos, not fruit.**

Your pancreas thanks you.

## 4. Fibre is a cornerstone of gut health — and the gut is connected to everything

Fibre isn’t a “nice to have.”

It’s the primary fuel for your gut microbiome — the trillions of organisms that influence immunity, inflammation, glucose control, mood, and even cognition.

The gut isn’t a tube; it’s an ecosystem.

And here’s the thing:

Most supermarket yogurts or “probiotic drinks” that brag about “live cultures” are selling a fantasy. Those cultures were alive when the product was manufactured — not weeks later in your fridge.

Dead cultures don’t fix your gut.\
Fiber does.

Soluble fiber feeds beneficial bacteria, which produce short-chain fatty acids like butyrate — compounds that reduce inflammation, protect the gut lining, support metabolic health, and indirectly help brain function.

The science on fiber and gut health is about as close to “case closed” as nutrition gets.

**If you want a healthy gut, feed it fiber — not marketing.**

## 5. Animal proteins are more complete — but plants can match them with trade-offs

Animal proteins contain essential amino acids in the ratios humans need — high leucine, high bioavailability, and strong digestibility.

Plant proteins work too — but usually require:

- higher total grams  
- combined sources (e.g., pea + rice)  
- more cost per gram of effective protein

Whey protein, for healthy adults, is one of the most studied and safest supplements in existence — beneficial for muscle synthesis, harmless to kidneys in healthy people, and extremely cost-efficient.

If your gut or ethics pull you toward plants, great — use blends.

**But scientifically, animal proteins are naturally more complete.**

Amino acids aren’t ideological.\
They’re biochemical.

## 6. Seed oils are not toxic — context matters

Seed oils — when used in ordinary cooking and not repeatedly overheated — are associated with lower LDL cholesterol and improved cardiovascular outcomes when they replace saturated fats.

The demonization of seed oils comes from guilty-by-association with ultra-processed foods, deep-frying, and snack foods overloaded with additives and simple carbs that wreck health independently.

SPL leans toward minimally processed fats where possible — olive oil, avocado oil, coconut oil, small amounts of butter/ghee, and cold-pressed oils. They’re simple, familiar, and usually come with fewer industrial steps between plant/animal and plate.

So the hierarchy is:

- minimise ultra-processed, deep-fried, repeatedly heated oils  
- prefer minimally processed / cold-pressed fats when you can  
- don’t panic if your reality sometimes means supermarket seed oils

Minimally processed oils? Better default.\
Deep-fried ultra-processed junk? Bad.

Context > fear.

_Many cheap seed oils are refined using industrial processes (including solvents like hexane). Those solvents are mostly removed and regulated to very low residual levels — they are considered safe — but from a common-sense standpoint, it’s reasonable to prefer oils that use simpler, less industrial processing when your budget and access allow it._

## 9. Supplements aren’t magic — they’re nutritional insurance for a world where food doesn’t give you what you think it does

In theory, you should get everything you need from food.\
In reality, almost nobody does.

Modern diets are predictable in their failures: low in omega-3, low in fiber, low in magnesium, low in vitamin D, low in potassium, low in phytonutrients, low in diversity, and built around convenience and stress. People skip vegetables. People don’t eat fruit. People rotate the same few foods for months.

So supplements aren’t “biohacks,” and they’re not magic.\
They’re gap-fillers — nothing more, nothing less.

They fill holes your modern life consistently leaves behind.

But the supplement industry is a circus.

Most products are under-dosed, contaminated, mislabeled, or simply useless.

A supplement is only as good as the company making it — and most companies are terrible.

So SPL’s position is straightforward:

Food first — that’s the ideal.\
But your real life doesn’t hit the ideal, so supplements become nutritional insurance.

Use only companies with third-party testing and transparent supply chains.\
Choose supplements that match your actual gaps — based on bloodwork, diet, and lifestyle, not influencer mood boards.

Supplements won’t save you.

**But the right supplements, made honestly, used correctly, keep your biology from collapsing under modern life.**

## 12. Strength training is essential for aging well

Muscle is the armor, engine, and insurance policy of aging.

It protects joints, stabilizes posture, drives metabolism, prevents falls, and preserves independence.

Without intentional strength training, muscle loss is automatic — accelerating every decade after 40.

**Strength training isn’t elective.**\
**It is the adult version of brushing your teeth.**

## 13. Tendons and ligaments adapt slower than muscle

Muscle gains happen in weeks.\
Tendon and ligament adaptation happens in months.

Especially after 40, the gap widens.

If your muscles grow faster than your connective tissue can handle, injuries follow.

This is why SPL emphasizes:

- isometrics  
- progressive overload  
- joint-first engineering  
- controlled movement  
- slow build-up

**You strengthen the chassis before you upgrade the engine.**

**Mobility is strength — just at awkward angles.**
