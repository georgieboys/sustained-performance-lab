# GP8 - Recovery Ritual

A down-regulation system for “leaving calmer than you arrived”, run at the end of every session.

---

# 1. OVERVIEW

Recovery Ritual is the fixed **exit block** for SPL.\
Every session has a clear end: we shift from “do work” to “stand down”.

Training, by design, pushes the system up: heart rate, tension, focus, alertness.\
If we stop there, people walk out wired, scattery, and more tightly wound than when they came in.

Recovery Ritual is a **small, high-leverage block**. It costs 5–8 minutes and minimal equipment, but compounds over time into better state control, safer loading, and a culture where performance and calm can coexist.

Recovery Ritual is how we **close the loop**:

- deliberate down-regulation  
- gentle movement  
- orienting vision  
- hydration as a closing cue  
- one last internal check-in

We standardise five components:

- Long exhales — deliberate parasympathetic bias via breathing.  
- Slow walking — gentle locomotion plus optic flow.  
- Soft gaze — widening visual field to signal safety.  
- Hydration — small, deliberate fluid intake as a “session is over” marker.  
- Internal scan — quick interoceptive check-in without storytelling.

You don’t need all five every time.\
Most sessions will use 2–3 components. On constrained days, we allow a **simple option**: choose 1–2 from the list, with a strong bias toward long exhales.

Global rule:

> You must leave calmer than you arrived.

---

# 2. PURPOSE

The Recovery Ritual Block is designed to:

- **Close physiological stress loops**\
Bring heart rate, breathing, and muscle tone back toward baseline after work.  
- **Shift autonomic state toward parasympathetic dominance**\
Longer exhales, soft gaze, and easy walking all push the system out of fight/flight.  
- **Protect the nervous system from cumulative “micro-frazzle”**\
Reduce the habit of stacking sessions on top of daily stress with no decompression.  
- **Train interoception without drama**\
Build the skill of noticing internal signals (“how am I actually doing?”) without spiralling into analysis.  
- **Create a consistent end-cap ritual**\
Give the brain a **clear flag** that training is over and recovery has begun, which supports habit formation and adherence.

Recovery Ritual is not a luxury “cool-down”.\
It’s a **structural safety feature** and a culture rule.

---

# 3. BENEFITS

**Autonomic downshift**

- Longer exhales and gentle movement can increase parasympathetic activity and lower sympathetic drive, especially after high-output or high-focus blocks.  
- Over time, this trains the ability to transition between “on” and “off” states on purpose.

**Reduced carry-over stress**

- Many people leave training still wired: shallow breathing, narrow focus, elevated tension.  
- Recovery Ritual lowers the chance that training _adds_ to daily stress load instead of helping to process it.

**Improved interoceptive clarity**

- A quick internal scan at the end of the session builds the habit of checking in:\
“What’s actually happening in my body right now?”  
- This helps people spot early warning signs (pain, overload, fatigue) before they become problems.

**Better adherence and emotional association**

- When sessions consistently **end in calm**, the body learns to associate training with regulation, not chaos.  
- That emotional memory makes it easier to keep showing up, especially for anxious or highly stressed individuals.

**Culture and coaching clarity**

- Recovery Ritual gives coaches a concrete, repeatable script for ending sessions.  
- It signals care: we don’t just push people; we help them land.

---

# 4. GLOBAL EXECUTION PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Placement & Duration

- Recovery Ritual runs at the **end of every session**.  
- Typical duration: **5–8 minutes**.  
- It is not optional; if time is compressed, we use the **simple option** (Section 10) rather than skipping it.

## 4.2 Components (The Menu)

We pull from five components:

- **Long exhales**\
Slow, controlled breathing with exhale longer than inhale.  
- **Slow walking**\
Gentle locomotion around the space; no phones, no rushing.  
- **Soft gaze**\
Panoramic, wide-angle visual mode instead of narrowed, target-focused eyes.  
- **Hydration**\
200–300 ml of water or light electrolytes, sipped slowly.  
- **Internal scan**\
Brief head-to-toe check-in, naming sensations in neutral language.

## 4.3 Minimum vs Full Protocol

We define three levels:

- **Minimum Dose (Time-Crunched)**  
    - At least **one** component.  
    - **Non-negotiable:** long exhales.  
    - Optional add: quick internal scan.  
- **Standard Session (Preferred)**  
    - **Two or three** components.  
    - Recommended combination:  
        - Long exhales  
        - Slow walking + soft gaze  
        - Hydration _or_ internal scan.  
- **Extended Reset (When Needed)**  
    - All five components.  
    - Useful after especially intense sessions, stressful days, or when state on entry was very dysregulated.

## 4.4 Breathing & Pace Rules

**Breathing**

- In through the nose where possible, out through nose or mouth.  
- Exhale is **longer** than inhale (e.g. 3–4 seconds in, 6–8 seconds out).  
- No deliberate breath-holding, no forced hyperventilation.

**Pace**

- Walking pace is casual, not purposeful.  
- Movements are smooth; nothing sharp or jittery.

## 4.5 “Leave Calmer Than You Arrived” Rule

At the end of Recovery Ritual, we want:

- Breathing quieter and easier than mid-session.  
- Muscle tone visibly lower (shoulders not glued to ears).  
- Gaze soft and easily panoramic.

Optional quick rating (self or coach prompt):

> “On a scale of 1–5, how calm do you feel right now compared to when you walked in?”\
> 1 = more wired, 3 = same, 5 = clearly calmer.

If someone consistently leaves at 1–2, we extend or adjust their ritual.

# 5. COMPONENT 1 — LONG EXHALES

## 5.1 Purpose

- Directly bias autonomic state toward parasympathetic activation.  
- Provide a simple, portable tool people can use outside the lab.  
- Mark the transition from “effort” to “closure”.

## 5.2 Standard Setup

- Position: standing, seated, or lying down.  
- Jaw relaxed; tongue resting lightly on the roof of the mouth if comfortable.  
- Shoulders relaxed; hands resting on thighs or by sides.

## 5.3 Protocol Levels

**Level 1 — Lite Exhale Set**

- Inhale gently for **3 seconds** through the nose.  
- Exhale **5–6 seconds** through nose or mouth.  
- Total: 8–10 breaths.

**Level 2 — Standard Recovery Set**

- Inhale **3–4 seconds** through the nose.  
- Exhale **6–8 seconds** through nose or mouth.  
- Total: 12–20 breaths (≈2–3 minutes).

**Level 3 — Extended Downshift**

- Maintain 3–4 in / 6–8 out for **3–5 minutes**.  
- Used selectively on high-stress days or after intense work.

Cue:\
“Quiet breath in, heavier breath out. Imagine you’re slowly deflating tension.”

---

# 6. COMPONENT 2 — SLOW WALKING

## 6.1 Purpose

- Use **gentle locomotion** to help circulation and downshift muscle tone.  
- Combine movement with optic flow: the environment moving past you, a strong safety signal.  
- Act as a decompression bridge between structured work and the outside world.

## 6.2 Standard Setup

- Environment: safe, uncluttered walking loop.  
- Posture: relaxed upright stance, arms swinging naturally.  
- Breathing: paired with long exhales protocol where possible.

## 6.3 Protocol Levels

**Level 1 — Short Loop**

- Duration: **2 minutes**.  
- Pace: slow stroll; you could easily hold a conversation.

**Level 2 — Standard Loop**

- Duration: **3–5 minutes**.  
- Maintain soft gaze (Component 3) and long exhales.

**Level 3 — Extended Walk-Out**

- Duration: **5–10 minutes**, often used as the path from lab to “real life” (e.g. walking home, to train, etc.).  
- Coaches may encourage this as “homework” after the on-site ritual is complete.

Cue:\
“Walk like you’re already done for the day, not like you’re late for the next thing.”

---

# 7. COMPONENT 3 — SOFT GAZE

## 7.1 Purpose

- Shift visual system from narrow, target-focused “threat mode” to wide, panoramic “we’re safe enough to explore” mode.  
- Support autonomic downshift and reduced cognitive tunnel vision.

## 7.2 Standard Setup

- Can be combined with slow walking or done standing/sitting.  
- Head level; eyes looking roughly at horizon height.

## 7.3 Protocol Levels

**Level 1 — Brief Panoramic Check**

- 30–60 seconds.  
- Let focus soften so you see the whole room at once.  
- No need to fixate on a single point.

**Level 2 — Paired with Walking**

- Maintain soft gaze for **2–3 minutes** while walking.  
- Let peripheral vision stay active: notice light, shapes, movement at the edges.

**Level 3 — Static + Breath Pair**

- Standing or sitting, eyes soft, 2–5 minutes.  
- Combine with long exhale protocol for compounded effect.

Cue:\
“Think horizon eyes, not sniper eyes.”

---

# 8. COMPONENT 4 — HYDRATION

## 8.1 Purpose

- Replace some fluid after training.  
- Use drinking as a **behavioural bookend**: “we are done now.”  
- Encourage calm, unhurried contact with the body (temperature, thirst, mouth feel).

## 8.2 Standard Setup

- Drink: water or light electrolyte mix.  
- Amount: **200–300 ml** as a guide, adjusted for context and preference.

## 8.3 Protocol Levels

**Level 1 — Quick Closing Sip**

- 3–5 slow sips.  
- Focus on the sensation: coolness, swallowing, breath between sips.

**Level 2 — Standard Hydration Cue**

- One small cup or bottle (≈200–300 ml).  
- Consumed over **1–2 minutes**, not chugged.

**Level 3 — Individualised Hydration**

- Adjusted based on session load, environment, and person’s broader hydration plan.  
- Still taken slowly, still used as a conscious closing cue.

Cue:\
“Let each sip be a small signal to your system: ‘We’re done.’”

---

# 9. COMPONENT 5 — INTERNAL SCAN

## 9.1 Purpose

- Build **non-judgemental body awareness**.  
- Catch issues early (pain, excessive fatigue, dizziness) before they’re hidden under busyness.  
- Reinforce that data from the body matters in decision-making.

## 9.2 Standard Setup

- Position: seated or standing; lying is also acceptable if safe.  
- Eyes closed or soft gaze.  
- Duration: **1–3 minutes**.

## 9.3 Protocol Levels

**Level 1 — Fast Label Scan**

- Scan from crown of head to soles of feet.  
- At each region, silently name one neutral word:  
    - “tight / loose / warm / cold / heavy / light / jittery / calm.”  
- No story, no interpretation.

**Level 2 — Structured Region Scan**

- Divide body into 4–6 zones:  
    - Head/neck, shoulders/arms, chest/back, hips/pelvis, knees, feet.  
- Spend ~10–20 seconds on each: notice contact, tension, pulse, temperature.  
- Optionally, finish by asking: “What’s one thing my body might need later today?”

**Level 3 — Scan + Log**

- Same as Level 2, plus a quick note in a log or app:  
    - One line: “Post-session state: [word], notes: [brief].”  
- Useful for tracking patterns over weeks (e.g. knees always stiff after certain blocks).

Cue:\
“Notice what’s there; don’t argue with it.”

---

# 10. SIMPLE MENU — CHOOSE ONE OR TWO

On days with limited time or attention, we run the **Simple Recovery Option**:

> **Minimum:**

- Do **long exhales** (Component 1, Level 1 or 2).

> **Better:**

- Long exhales **plus one** of:  
    - Slow walking  
    - Soft gaze  
    - Hydration  
    - Internal scan

Even in Simple Mode, the rule stands:

> You must leave calmer than you arrived.

# 11. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION — SUMMARY

**Autonomic regulation**

- Controlled breathing with **extended exhalation** influences vagal tone and cardiorespiratory coupling, supporting shifts toward parasympathetic dominance and reduced physiological arousal.

**Vision and locomotion**

- **Optic flow** during slow walking and panoramic gaze patterns are linked with reduced threat signalling and improved state regulation compared with narrow, target-locked vision in threat contexts.

**Interoception and awareness**

- Regular, low-drama interoceptive check-ins improve body awareness and can support better load management, emotional regulation, and earlier detection of overload or injury.

**Hydration and ritual cues**

- Moderate post-exercise fluid intake supports recovery and, when paired with a consistent sequence, becomes a **behavioural marker** that helps the brain delineate “session vs life”.
