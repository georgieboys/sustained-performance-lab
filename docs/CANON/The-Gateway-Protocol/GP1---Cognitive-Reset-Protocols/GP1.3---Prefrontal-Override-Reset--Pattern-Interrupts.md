# GP1.3 - Prefrontal Override Reset — Pattern Interrupts

_A short cognitive task that breaks repetitive thought patterns and restores attentional flexibility._

---

# 1. MECHANISM

Pattern Interrupts shift the brain into a different cognitive mode by introducing a rapid task switch.

This activates external-attention networks, occupies working memory, and re-engages executive control, briefly interrupting cognitive loops.

---

# 2. PRIMARY PURPOSE

To quickly break a stuck thought pattern and restore enough cognitive flexibility to continue the Gateway Protocol.

---

# 3. WHEN TO USE IT

Use a Pattern Interrupt when:\
• attention remains scattered or unresponsive\
• overthinking interferes with task transitions\
• motivation is stalled\
• internal noise blocks Dynamic Flow or Isometrics\
• cognitive spirals feel repetitive and non-emotional\
• the mind feels “stuck” and uncooperative

Pattern Interrupts are optional. They are used only when a cognitive break is needed.

---

# 4. WHO RESPONDS BEST

This modality is effective for:

• analytical or logical thinkers\
• verbal or linguistic processors\
• visual-pattern or detail-oriented users\
• tactile or hands-on regulators\
• novelty-seeking or fast-switching brains\
• ADHD-leaning individuals\
• users who prefer external focus over introspection\
• individuals who regulate well through shared, silent tasks

---

# 5. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION

Pattern Interrupts are supported by established cognitive mechanisms:

• **task switching** decreases default-mode activity and interrupts rumination\
• **attentional redeployment** reduces cognitive load by shifting focus externally\
• **working-memory occupation** displaces repetitive thought loops\
• **executive re-engagement** restores cognitive control\
• **visual search** activates external attention networks\
• **micro-flow states** reduce cognitive noise\
• **shared attention** supports co-regulation

Pattern Interrupts are brief cognitive diversions, not therapeutic interventions.

---

# 6. THE SPL PROTOCOL

SPL Pattern Interrupts operate through **two pathways**:

1. **Tactile Pattern Interrupts**  
1. **Digital Pattern Interrupts**

Users choose whichever pathway feels natural.\
No instructions. No completion. No structure.\
Engage for a few minutes until the mind resets.

---

## A. TACTILE PATTERN INTERRUPTS

Physical puzzles that shift cognition through logic, language, or visual search.

**1\. Logical Puzzles (Sudoku)**\
**2\. Verbal Puzzles (Crosswords)**\
**3\. Visual Puzzles (Pattern Recognition)**\
**4\. Small Jigsaw Puzzles**\
**5\. Large Communal Jigsaw**

These tools work for users who regulate well through tactile engagement or slow visual tasks.

---

## B. DIGITAL PATTERN INTERRUPTS

Short cognitive resets delivered through a dedicated game tablet.

**1\. Fast Games (high engagement)**\
Call of Duty: Mobile, PUBG, Tetris, racing games, match-based games.

**2\. Logic and Visual Games**\
Digital Sudoku, pattern-match apps, hidden-object apps.

**3\. Immersive Visual Modules**\
Low-interaction visual apps for calm, steady focus.

Users simply pick something and play.\
The cognitive shift happens automatically.

---

# 7. THE 15-MINUTE VERSION

A 15-minute Pattern Interrupt is simple:

**Choose a tactile or digital task and engage with it for up to 15 minutes.**\
**Move on when you’re ready.**

There is no structure to follow.\
The reset comes from task switching, not task completion.

---

# 8. SHORT VERSIONS

Short versions remain for consistency with other SPL modalities:

Pick any tactile or digital task and engage briefly until the loop breaks.

---

# 9. FULL SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION

Pattern Interrupts rely on multiple well-supported mechanisms in cognitive neuroscience:

---

## 1. Task Switching Suppresses Rumination

Rumination depends on default-mode network (DMN) activity.\
Externally oriented tasks activate task-positive networks (TPN), suppressing DMN activity.\
The interruption is immediate.

---

## 2. Attentional Redeployment Lowers Cognitive Load

Redirecting attention to an external task reduces emotional salience and cognitive spiraling.\
This mechanism is used in behavioural activation and attention-shift interventions.

---

## 3. Working-Memory Occupation Disrupts Repetitive Thought

Puzzles, games, and visual search engage verbal, visuospatial, or executive working-memory subsystems.\
Rumination cannot run in parallel.

---

## 4. Executive Re-Engagement Restores Control

Structured tasks recruit prefrontal networks required for cognitive control, decision readiness, and task organisation.

---

## 5. Visual Search Activates External Attention Networks

Hidden-object tasks and jigsaws activate parietal and occipital circuits, reducing internal narrative load.

---

## 6. Micro-Flow States Reduce Cognitive Interference

Short, structured play tasks create clear goals and immediate feedback, inducing micro-flow conditions that reduce cognitive noise.

---

## 7. Social Co-Regulation Through Shared Silent Tasks

Parallel engagement with the communal jigsaw provides predictable social cues and downregulates autonomic arousal.

---

## 8. Behavioural Loop Interruption

The cognitive logic matches loop-breaking models used across behavioural science:\
interrupt → redirect → reset.
