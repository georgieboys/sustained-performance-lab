# GP9 - Thermal Reset

A water-based closing ritual for the Gateway Protocol, built on the shower.

---

# 1. OVERVIEW

THERMAL RESET is the **end-cap of the Gateway Protocol**.

You finish your Cognitive Reset and movement work, then you **end the whole thing with water**:

- **Base:** take a shower.  
- **If possible:** hot shower → then finish with the coldest / coolest water you can manage.  
- **If no shower:** pour water over your head and body from a bucket / pail / tap.

That’s the whole spine of the system.

From there, **everything else is just an upgrade**:

- Heat upgrade: **sauna / sauna tent**.  
- Cold upgrade: **ice face dunk or ice bath**.

Buckets, cheap showers, fancy saunas, thousand-dollar ice tubs — they’re all just different skins on that same closing move.

---

# 2. PURPOSE

Inside the Gateway Protocol, THERMAL RESET is designed to:

- **Close the loop**\
You don’t just drift away from your session; you end it with a clear physical ritual: _“I did my work, now I wash it off.”_  
- **Standardise thermal rhythm**  
    - Warm/hot part → vasodilation, relaxation, muscle comfort.  
    - Cold finish → sharp signal, vascular “pump”, clean end-state.  
- **Make the Gateway feel “complete”**\
The shower is the punctuation mark. When the water stops, you’re done.  
- **Keep it universally accessible**\
If you have a tap and a container, you can do a minimal version. If you have a shower, you can do the SPL standard. If you have sauna / ice bath, you layer them on top.

---

# 3. BENEFITS

Used consistently at the end of Gateway Protocol, THERMAL RESET:

- **Creates a reliable habit hook**\
“Gateway → shower” is binary and easy to remember. That makes the whole system stickier than “Gateway → maybe something later”.  
- **Supports vasodilation → cold finish pattern**\
Heating up first, then finishing cold, gives a simple vascular and autonomic swing without needing extreme protocols.  
- **Helps emotional and cognitive decompression**\
The sensory change of water + temperature is a blunt but effective way to leave “effort mode” and re-enter normal life.

SPL stance: it’s a **behavioural anchor first**, physiology bonus second.

---

# 4. EXECUTION PRINCIPLES

## 4.1 Placement in the Day

- THERMAL RESET is **always last** in the Gateway sequence.  
- Once per Gateway day: do your work, then do your water.

## 4.2 Dose & Feel

- Aim for **noticeable but not extreme**:  
    - Warm/hot: enough to feel loosened but not dizzy.  
    - Cold finish: enough to make you breathe a bit harder at first, but within 10–15 seconds you can control your breathing.  
- You should leave the bathroom thinking:  
    - “That was a clear reset,”\
not, “That was a near-death experience.”

# 5. LEVELS & MODALITIES

## 5.1 BASE LEVEL — WATER RITUAL (MINIMUM VIABLE THERMAL RESET)

For contexts with limited infrastructure.

**If you don’t have a reliable shower:**

- End your Gateway work.  
- Fill a bucket / pail / large container with **any available water** (room temperature is fine).  
- Pour water over your head, neck, and upper body **3–5 times**.  
- Feel the sensory change and mentally tag it: _“Session is over.”_  
- Towel off, change clothes if needed, re-enter your day.

This keeps the pattern alive anywhere: **water on head = Gateway complete**.

If you _do_ have a shower but only one temperature, you can treat a quick rinse as this base level.

---

## 5.2 LEVEL 1 — SPL STANDARD SHOWER (HOT → COLD)

This is the **assumed standard** for most people.

**Sequence:**

1. **Warm / hot phase**

    - Turn the shower to a comfortably hot level.  
    - Stand under it for **3–5 minutes**.  
    - Let muscles relax, breathing stay easy.

1. **Cold / cool finish**

    - Turn the tap to the coldest / coolest setting you can tolerate.  
    - Stay under it for **30–60 seconds**.  
    - Focus on long exhales and keeping your eyes soft.

1. Step out, towel off, and consciously note: _“Session is done for today.”_

That’s it. Do this, and your THERMAL RESET is “on spec” for SPL.

---

## 5.3 LEVEL 2 — HEAT UPGRADE (SAUNA)

If you have access to a sauna / sauna tent, it becomes an **upgrade to the heat phase**, not a replacement for the shower anchor.

**Example sequence:**

1. End Gateway work.  
1. **Sauna:**

    - 8–15 minutes at a tolerable temperature.  
    - Sit or lie down, calm breathing, no phones.

1. **Shower:**

    - Brief warm/hot rinse (1–3 minutes).  
    - Finish with **30–60 seconds cold** as per Level 1.

You can flex the sauna time, but the logic stays:

> Work → Heat (sauna) → Shower (hot → cold) → Done.

## 5.4 LEVEL 2 — COLD UPGRADE (FACE DUNK / ICE BATH)

These are upgrades to the **cold end** of the ritual.

### 5.4.1 Ice Face Dunk

Low volume, sharp stimulus.

- After the warm/hot phase (shower or sauna), prepare a basin with cold water + ice.  
- Take a normal breath.  
- Dunk your face (down to the cheeks) for **5–15 seconds**.  
- Come up, breathe calmly 30–60 seconds.  
- Repeat **2–3 times**.

You can combine this with:

- A standard Level-1 shower (hot → cold), or  
- A quick rinse after the face dunk if you used it standalone.

### 5.4.2 Ice Bath / Cold Plunge (Advanced)

For people with access and medical clearance.

- Finish your main work and heat phase (if any).  
- Enter the cold tub up to chest / shoulders.  
- Stay **1–3 minutes**, keeping breathing under control.  
- Exit, towel off, dress warm, walk around 10–15 minutes.  
- You may still choose to do a very short shower (even just a quick rinse) to anchor the ritual.

From an SPL perspective, this is strictly **optional**. The system works perfectly well at Base and Level 1.

# 6. SAFETY

- Check with a clinician before strong heat or cold if you have:  
    - Cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or arrhythmias  
    - Frequent fainting / unexplained dizziness  
    - Late-stage pregnancy  
    - Severe respiratory or peripheral vascular issues  
- Universal rules:  
    - No alcohol / sedatives with sauna or ice bath.  
    - No breath-hold games in any water.  
    - Stop immediately if you feel chest pain, tunnel vision, nausea, or confusion.

The right dose is the one where you could have done slightly more, but choose not to.
