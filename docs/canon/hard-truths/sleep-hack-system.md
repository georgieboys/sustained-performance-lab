### For the Average Urban Legend in Singapore

You live in Singapore.\
Life is loud, fast, bright, and humid. Your calendar is full, your brain is full, your phone is _too_ full.

You already know sleep matters. You don’t need another sermon about “8 hours or else”.\
You need a **forgiving system** that:

- Works in a high-stress, urban environment  
- Uses tools you actually have (aircon, shower, cheap supplements, a book)  
- Asks for **one hard habit change**, not 27

This is the **SPL Sleep Hack System – Level 1**\
Built for the average urban legend, not a full-time athlete or monk.

---

## STEP 1 – Build Your Cave

Before we talk hours or hacks, fix **where** you sleep.

If you can’t control your life, control your cave.

### 1.1 Light – One Warm Lamp

At night, your brain should get a simple signal: **“Day is over.”**

- In the bedroom at night, use **one small, warm light** (bedside lamp, warm LED).  
- Avoid ceiling-bright interrogation lighting.  
- If you must touch your phone: brightness low, quick, no doom-scroll.

**Rule:**

> When the warm lamp is on, the day is closing.\
> When it goes off, your brain knows: we’re done.

---

### 1.2 Temperature – Cold Cave, Warm Shell

Singapore superpower = aircon. Use it properly.

- Set the room so that **without a blanket, you feel slightly cold**.  
- With your blanket, you feel comfortably cosy.  
- Fan + AC is fine; it’s the **overall chill** that matters, not a specific number.

Your body likes a small **drop in core temperature** before sleep.\
So we build a **cold cave, warm shell**.

---

### 1.3 Cocoon – One Good Blanket

Pick **one main blanket**:

- Breathable  
- Not paper-thin, not a winter duvet from Hokkaido  
- Has a _little_ weight to it

That blanket lives on your bed and nowhere else.\
Your nervous system learns:

> “This blanket = shutting down for the day.”

Cave done.

---

## STEP 2 – Take a Warm Shower Before Bed

You don’t need hacks yet. You need water.

**What to do:**

- 10–15 minutes under a **comfortably warm shower**  
- About **60–90 minutes before you’d like to sleep**

You warm the skin → dump heat after → core cools → body reads:

> “Nice, it’s sleep time.”

Make it the start of your shut-down sequence.

---

## STEP 3 – Magnesium as Training Wheels (Not a Life Sentence)

Magnesium is a **good entry-level tool**, especially if your sleep is currently trash.

But we treat it as **training wheels**, not a lifelong dependency.

### 3.1 Why we use it

- Helps with muscle relaxation and down-regulation  
- Can improve sleep quality and time-to-fall-asleep in people with poor sleep  
- Cheap and generally well-tolerated for healthy adults

### 3.2 How to use it (Phase 1 – Getting Started)

- Form: **magnesium glycinate / bisglycinate / L-threonate**  
- Dose: about **200–300 mg elemental magnesium**  
- Timing: **60–90 minutes before bed**, ideally with a bit of food

Layer it on top of:

> Cave → Warm shower → Magnesium → Cognitive Reset → Book → Sleep

### 3.3 Big caveat: you should be able to wean off

The science is clear: magnesium helps most when sleep is **already bad**.\
Once your sleep is consistently good, the extra benefit is small.

**SPL Position:**

- Use magnesium to help stabilise your **early sleep practice**.  
- As your sleep becomes reliable (cave + shower + reset + book), you **test nights without it**.  
- The long-term ideal:

> **Shower → Cognitive Reset → Book → Sleep**\
> without needing magnesium every night.

_(If you have kidney disease or complex medication, check with a doc before playing with magnesium.)_

---

## STEP 4 – Weekly Forgiveness: One Alarmless Morning

You’re going to have weeks of **5–6 hours** a night.\
Kids, deadlines, shift work, nonsense. That’s real life.

Instead of pretending you’ll suddenly become an 8-hour robot, we build in **forgiveness**.

### 4.1 On workdays: don’t amputate your sleep

On normal days:

- Do what you must.  
- But don’t voluntarily turn a **5–6 hour night into a 3–4 hour night** by scrolling, gaming, or doom-reading.

If you can realistically be in bed for 6 hours, **protect those 6**.

No guilt, just **“don’t make it worse.”**

---

### 4.2 Once a week: no alarm, just body

Pick **one morning a week** (usually Saturday or Sunday):

- No alarm  
- No 8am brunch  
- No early call if you can avoid it

You sleep in your cave, then:

- Let your body decide when it’s finished  
- Notice roughly how long that was  
- Notice how you feel

That’s your **weekly bailout**.\
You don’t owe your body perfection. You owe it this.

---

## STEP 5 – Creatine for “The Morning After”

You had a bad night. You still need to function.

Creatine won’t replace sleep, but it can keep your brain **less useless** when you’re short.

### 5.1 What it does

- Acts as an **energy buffer** for brain and muscle  
- Helps especially when the system is under stress: heavy training, sleep loss, high cognitive load

### 5.2 How to use it

Simplest move:

- Take **3–5 g creatine monohydrate once a day**, ideally **every day**:  
    - With breakfast or your first proper meal

Over a few weeks, your brain and muscles saturate creatine stores.\
Then on sleep-deprived days, you have a bit more **metabolic resilience**.

Think of it as:

> “Urban brain armour,”\
> not a licence to sleep 3 hours.

- **EXTREME HACK: 15-25g creatine monohydrate on a really bad morning where you have had less than 4 hours of sleep. **  
    - Science based protocol but please use with caution and dont make it a habit.

_(Kidney disease / complex medical history = talk to a clinician first.)_

---

## STEP 6 – Cognitive Reset: The SPL Bridge into Sleep

Warm shower sets the body. Magnesium (if you’re using it) smooths the edges.\
Now you need to **change state**.

For SPL people, this is where **Cognitive Reset** is our cheat code.

We are **not** using all six pathways here.\
For **pre-sleep**, we recommend **four** and put explicit caveats on the other two.

### 6.1 Recommended for Sleep (Pick ONE)

You’re aiming for **5–15 minutes** after your shower / magnesium, before your book.

Ask:

> “What’s the loudest thing right now – body, thoughts, or feelings?”

Then match:

#### A. Breathwork Reset – for a wired body

Use when: **physically wired**, tense, heart-rate up.

- 5–10 mins of **slow, simple breathing**, e.g.:  
    - Inhale 4 seconds → exhale 6 seconds  
    - Through nose, relaxed  
- Done lying in bed or on a mat.

Goal: turn the dial down on your autonomic system.

---

#### B. Brain Dump Reset – for spinning thoughts

Use when: **to-do loops and worries** won’t shut up.

- 5–10 minutes with pen + paper  
- Dump:  
    - All tasks and errands for tomorrow / the week  
    - Any “don’t forget this” items

Close the notebook. That’s where your “tomorrow” lives now, not in your head.

---

#### C. Somatic Discharge Reset – for a braced body

Use when: **you feel tight, locked, hunched**, or like you’ve been bracing all day.

- Run the simple 3-pose sequence:

    1. **Child’s Pose** – 1–2 minutes  
    1. **Forward Fold** (standing or seated) – 1–2 minutes  
    1. **Supine Twist** each side – 1–2 minutes

Slow, easy, breathing. No stretch heroics. Just letting tension leak out.

---

#### D. Creative Output Reset – for emotional sludge

Use when: **mood is foggy / heavy**, but not sharp anxiety.

- 5–15 minutes of **low-stakes creating**:  
    - Doodle  
    - Colour  
    - Sketch nonsense  
    - Write 10 lines of “whatever comes out” in a notebook

No judgement, no “good art”. Just externalising the internal mess a little.

---

### 6.2 Caveats: Pattern Interrupt & Coregulation Before Bed

We **do NOT recommend** these two as standard pre-sleep tools.

#### Pattern Interrupt Reset – why not at bedtime?

- Puzzles, games, problem-solving → great for breaking obsessive loops _earlier_ in the day.  
- Close to sleep, they can:  
    - Create new cognitive loops  
    - Keep your brain active and analytical  
    - Delay sleep, not help it

**SPL rule for sleep:**

> Pattern Interrupt is for daytime / early evening loop-breaking,\
> not for the last 30–60 minutes before bed.

---

#### Coregulation Reset – very limited use at bedtime

Coregulation (using another person’s nervous system to settle) is powerful, _but_:

- It depends on **other people**  
- It can easily drift into **high-stakes, emotionally charged conversations**  
- Emotional processing at 11:30 pm often → _more_ activation, not less

So for sleep we say:

- Coregulation is **not a primary pre-sleep tool**.  
- If it happens:  
    - Keep it _very_ low-stakes  
    - No big fights, no relationship summits, no life admin marathons  
    - Think: casual, gentle chat, not therapy session

But officially, for the **Sleep Hack System**, we recommend **Breathwork, Brain Dump, Somatic, Creative** as the four go-to Cognitive Resets.

---

## STEP 7 – The One Hard Habit: Book, Not Phone

This is the big one.

You can have the best cave, shower, supplements and resets, and still wreck it all with **30–60 minutes of phone chaos** at the end.

So we keep one non-negotiable rule:

### The One-Book Rule

> **For the last 30 minutes before you intend to sleep, your phone is done.**\
> **A physical book (or e-ink) comes into bed instead.**

Practical:

- Pick a rough “sleep time” in your head (say, midnight).  
- At 11:30 pm:  
    - Put the phone on the table / floor / another room.  
    - No more scrolling or “just one more check”.  
- Get into bed with **one book**:  
    - Any genre, zero notifications, zero infinite scroll.  
- Read until your eyes get heavy. Light off. Sleep.

If you change **only one behaviour**, change this one.

---

## STEP 8 – Optional: Evening Ice Bath (High-Performance Only)

This is **not** part of the base system.\
This is for people who:

- Already train hard  
- Already use cold exposure  
- Have no heart issues / major medical red flags  
- Have access to a tub / plunge / chiller

If that’s you:

- Water temp: **~10–15°C** (13°C sweet spot)  
- Duration: **2–5 minutes**  
- Timing: **about 1–2 hours before bed**, not straight-into-bed  
- Then:  
    - Dry off  
    - Warm clothes  
    - Light snack if needed  
    - Warm lamp + Cognitive Reset + Book

Treat it as a **recovery adjunct**, not a required sleep tool.

## The Ideal End State

Where we ultimately want you to land, once training wheels are off:

> **Cave built → Warm shower → ONE Cognitive Reset → Book, not phone → Sleep.**

Magnesium becomes optional.\
Creatine is your background insurance.\
Ice baths are extra credit for maniacs.

The system is not here to make you perfect.\
It’s here to make you **less wrecked, more often**, in a city trying its best to keep you wired.

## Scientific validation

Here’s how this stack maps onto current evidence:

- **Light & “book not phone”:**  
    - Evening use of light-emitting e-readers delays melatonin, pushes circadian timing later, and worsens next-morning alertness compared with print books.  
    - Similar effects have been shown with tablets: evening tablet use delays self-selected bedtimes, suppresses melatonin and impairs sleep.  
- **Warm shower before bed:**  
    - A 2019 systematic review/meta-analysis found that warm baths/showers (40–42.5°C for ~10–15 min, 1–2 h before bed) shortened sleep onset and improved sleep efficiency and total sleep time.  
    - A large home-based study in older adults showed hot-water bathing 1–3 h before bed was associated with shorter time to fall asleep.  
- **Magnesium as a starter tool:**  
    - A double-blind RCT in older adults with insomnia found magnesium improved insomnia scores, sleep efficiency, total sleep time and sleep onset latency.  
    - Newer RCTs with magnesium bisglycinate and magnesium-L-threonate in adults with poor sleep show improvements in sleep quality, duration, deep sleep and readiness vs placebo.  
- **Weekly “no alarm” catch-up:**  
    - Observational studies show that among adults sleeping <6 h on weekdays, modest weekend catch-up sleep is associated with lower prevalence of metabolic syndrome and better metabolic markers.  
    - Newer device-based work suggests weekend catch-up doesn’t magically erase CVD risk and that sleep **regularity** also matters, which fits your framing of this as partial damage control, not a cure.  
- **Creatine for the sleep-deprived brain:**  
    - A 2024 trial found that a single high dose of creatine during 21 h of sleep deprivation improved cognitive performance and altered brain high-energy phosphate status.  
    - Reviews of creatine and cognition conclude that supplementation increases brain creatine and may confer benefits on memory, attention and processing speed, especially under metabolic stress.  
    - General clinical reviews support standard-dose creatine monohydrate as safe in healthy adults, with kidney disease as the main caution group.  
- **Breathwork before bed:**  
    - Slow breathing / HRV-biofeedback sessions before sleep have improved sleep disturbance scores and HRV indices in small RCTs.  
    - Broader work on slow diaphragmatic breathing supports its use as an adjunct treatment for insomnia via autonomic down-regulation.  
- **Brain Dump / pre-sleep to-do list:**  
    - A lab study showed that 5 minutes of writing a specific to-do list before bed led to faster sleep onset vs writing about completed tasks; more detailed lists were linked to faster sleep.  
- **Somatic / relaxation work:**  
    - Progressive muscle relaxation protocols consistently improve sleep quality and reduce fatigue in various clinical groups (e.g. cardiac surgery patients, menopausal women, kids on chemo).  
    - Your 3-pose somatic sequence sits in the same general family of body-based relaxation tools that down-shift arousal.  
- **Cold water immersion as advanced adjunct:**  
    - In well-trained runners, whole-body or partial cold water immersion after high-intensity evening exercise altered thermoregulation and recovery kinetics without harming subsequent sleep architecture.  
    - Reviews on cold water immersion suggest possible benefits for recovery, perceived sleep quality and wellbeing, but emphasise small sample sizes and limited RCTs.  
    - One evening-exercise + CWI study specifically concluded that post-exercise CWI did **not** negatively affect athletes’ subsequent sleep.

Overall: the core of the system (light, warm shower, magnesium as starter, weekly catch-up, slow breathing, journaling / brain dump, relaxation, book not phone) is very well aligned with current evidence. Creatine and cold plunges are correctly framed as **adjuncts** for specific use-cases, not sleep replacements.
