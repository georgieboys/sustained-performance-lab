Most nutrition systems start with the food:

- Eat X grams of protein  
- Hit Y calories  
- Avoid Z ingredients

They assume:

> “If the plan is correct, behaviour will follow.”

SPL calls nonsense on that.

People rarely fail because they don’t know what to eat.\
They fail because of how **biology, brain wiring, and behaviour** collide with **real life**.

Archetypes exist purely to **improve nutrition outcomes**:

- they tell us **which levers** to pull for each person,  
- they help us **avoid** the wrong intervention (more rules for a Controller, “trust your hunger” for a Ghost in RED-S, etc.).

---

### 1. SCIENTIFIC FOUNDATIONS: DIMENSIONS & TESTS

SPL’s behavioural layer uses five well-validated constructs from eating-behaviour research:

1. **Awareness / Interoception**  
1. **Cognitive Restraint**  
1. **Emotional Eating**  
1. **External & Uncontrolled Eating**  
1. **Intuitive Eating**

Each has:

- a clear conceptual definition  
- validated psychometric tools  
- known associations with outcomes (weight, diet quality, ED risk, wellbeing)  
- known limitations

SPL doesn’t invent these; we compress them into something usable.

---

#### 1.1 Awareness / Interoception (Mindful & Attuned Eating)

**Concept**

Ability to:

- detect internal cues (hunger, fullness, energy shifts)  
- distinguish them from emotional or situational states  
- respond appropriately most of the time

**Key Tools**

- **Mindful Eating Questionnaire (MEQ)** – multiple subscales including Awareness, Distraction, Emotional, External Cues, Disinhibition; decent internal reliability.  
- **Mindful Eating Behavior Scale (MEBS)** – focused on conscious attention to eating; subscales for focused eating, hunger/fullness cues, etc., with good psychometrics.  
- Lab interoception tasks (e.g. heartbeat detection) inform the theory but are not practical in SPL.

**What it predicts**

Higher mindful/aware eating is associated with:

- less impulsive eating  
- lower disinhibition  
- sometimes better weight and eating outcomes

**SPL usage**

We use short, structured questions modelled on MEQ/MEBS to estimate:

- how early someone notices hunger/fullness  
- how often they eat on autopilot  
- how they eat under distraction

Awareness is trainable, but modulated by physiology and neurodivergence.

---

#### 1.2 Cognitive Restraint (Dieting Mindset)

**Concept**

Degree and style of deliberate control over eating to manage weight/shape/health.

- High vs low restraint  
- **Flexible** vs **rigid** restraint

**Key Tools**

- **Three-Factor Eating Questionnaire (TFEQ / TFEQ-R18/21)** – Cognitive Restraint, Uncontrolled Eating, Emotional Eating.  
- **Dutch Eating Behavior Questionnaire (DEBQ)** – Restrained Eating subscale.  
- **Rigid vs Flexible Control scales** – separate items into rigid (“never”, “forbidden”) vs flexible (“sometimes”, “portion control”).

**What it predicts**

- High **rigid** restraint + high **Uncontrolled Eating** = worst outcomes (binges, yo-yo dieting).  
- Flexible restraint generally supports longer-term success.

**SPL usage**

We care about _flavour_ more than absolute height:

- Do they have lots of “always/never” rules?  
- Do slips trigger “I’ve ruined it” spirals?  
- Can they integrate favourite foods sanely?

We aim to move people toward **flexible restraint** if restraint is needed at all.

---

#### 1.3 Emotional Eating

**Concept**

Using food primarily to regulate **emotional states** (negative or positive), rather than physical hunger.

**Key Tools**

- **DEBQ Emotional Eating** – desire to eat when feeling down, irritated, lonely etc.  
- **Emotional Eating Scale (EES)** – urges to eat under specific emotions (anger, anxiety, depression).  
- **Emotional Appetite Questionnaire (EMAQ)** – changes in appetite under different emotional conditions.

**What it predicts**

High emotional eating scores are associated with:

- higher BMI  
- more binge episodes  
- more depressive and anxious symptoms  
- poorer weight-management outcomes

**Caveats**

Self-report “emotional eating” doesn’t always mean lab-measured higher intake under induced mood; scales can reflect:

- beliefs about why one overeats  
- general disinhibition

**SPL usage**

High emotional eating flags:

- heavier reliance on food for regulation  
- higher risk for Soother / Reactor patterns

We still run triage: **hunger** and **rigid restraint** are checked first.

---

#### 1.4 External & Uncontrolled Eating

**Concept**

- **External eating** – eating in response to cues: sight, smell, presence of food, social context.  
- **Uncontrolled eating** – subjective loss of control and overshooting intake.

**Key Tools**

- **DEBQ External Eating** – cue-driven eating tendencies.  
- **TFEQ Uncontrolled Eating** – loss of control, overeating.  
- **Power of Food Scale (PFS)** – perceived “power” of food in the environment.

**What it predicts**

High external/uncontrolled eating is associated with:

- higher snack frequency  
- higher energy intake  
- higher BMI and obesity risk  
- greater reward response to food cues in neuroimaging studies

**SPL usage**

High scores point toward **Environment-Driven** and **Reactor** patterns:

- we target environment design, friction, and anchor meals  
- we treat the food environment as an **active force**, not neutral background

---

#### 1.5 Intuitive Eating

**Concept**

A more adaptive pattern characterised by:

- eating in response to hunger and fullness  
- eating primarily for physical needs  
- **unconditional permission** to eat all foods  
- aligning choices with health and performance over time

**Key Tool**

**Intuitive Eating Scale-2 (IES-2)** – four subscales:

- Unconditional Permission to Eat  
- Eating for Physical Rather Than Emotional Reasons  
- Reliance on Hunger and Satiety Cues  
- Body–Food Choice Congruence

**What it predicts**

Higher intuitive eating is associated with:

- lower disordered eating  
- better body image  
- more stable weight  
- better psychological wellbeing  
- improved cardiometabolic outcomes in some interventions, even without large weight loss

**Caveats**

- Most evidence is observational.  
- Signals may be unreliable in chronic dieters or RED-S; they might need structure before “trust your body” is safe.  
- High-performance sport or extreme aesthetic goals often require periodised override of cues.

**SPL usage**

We treat intuitive eating traits as the **direction of travel** in Base Mode, not as a universal requirement in every phase.

### 2. FROM DIMENSIONS TO ARCHETYPES

Research using TFEQ, DEBQ, IES-2 and related tools repeatedly finds clusters:

- High **Restraint** + high **Uncontrolled** + high **Emotional** → “unsuccessful dieters” / binge-prone.  
- Low restraint + low uncontrolled → more **regulated** eaters.  
- High **External/Uncontrolled** → impulsive/hedonic eaters.

Typologies like **CSIRO diet types** (Thinker, Craver, Foodie, Socialiser, Freewheeler) summarise broader behavioural/personality patterns.

SPL archetypes are our practical mapping of these empirical patterns onto:

- the **five dimensions** we track, and  
- a system that plugs into **triage** and **nutrition planning**.

Archetypes are **stateful patterns**:

- A person might be Controller at work, Environment-Driven at parties, Ghost under high workload.

We expect contextual shifts and use archetypes as **maps**, not identity boxes.

### 3. CORE SPL EATING ARCHETYPES

**Core SPL archetypes (one-liners)**

- **Regulated / Intuitive** – basically stable; can self-regulate without heavy tools.  
- **Controller** – loves rules, tracking, numbers; can drift into rigidity.  
- **Soother** – uses food to blunt emotion / stress.  
- **Reactor** – high restraint + high loss of control; out-of-control episodes, often after restriction.  
- **Environment-Driven** – eats whatever is around; defaults to convenience and cues.  
- **Ghost** – under-eats chronically / forgets to eat; low appetite or high distraction.

Each archetype:

- is defined by a dimensional profile  
- has a scientific analogue in the literature  
- comes with risks  
- guides which nutrition levers we pull

**Before using archetypes, SPL always runs the triage gate: hardware and neurology first. If someone sits in clear clinical ED / RED-S or severe psychiatric territory, archetypes are ****_not_**** the primary tool.**

#### 3.1 Regulated / Intuitive Eater

> **One-line:** basically stable; can self-regulate without heavy tools.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: high  
- Restraint: low–moderate, **flexible**  
- Emotional: low  
- External/Uncontrolled: low–moderate  
- Intuitive: high

**Analogue**

- High IES-2  
- Low DEBQ Emotional/External  
- Low TFEQ Uncontrolled  
- Shows up as “regulated/intuitive” clusters.

**Risks**

- Nutrient gaps (especially protein and fibre).  
- Drift into more chaotic patterns during high stress or environment change.

**SPL focus**

- Protect relationship with food.  
- Layer in basic performance fuelling (protein, carbs around training) without creating excessive rigidity.  
- Keep this as the **Base Mode anchor**.

---

#### 3.2 Controller

> **One-line:** loves rules, tracking, numbers; can drift into rigidity.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: medium–high, often overridden by rules  
- Restraint: high, tends toward **rigid**  
- Emotional: low–medium (often suppressed)  
- External/Uncontrolled: low most of the time; spikes after “failures”  
- Intuitive: low

**Analogue**

- High TFEQ/DEBQ Restraint, especially **rigid** control.  
- Often moderate Uncontrolled and Emotional in “unsuccessful dieter” clusters.

**Risks**

- Weight cycling, binges, burnout from constant rules.  
- Slipping into Reactor mode when restriction is pushed too hard.

**SPL focus**

- Shift toward **flexible restraint**:  
    - soften rules  
    - design graceful error recovery  
- Ensure no aggressive deficits that trigger **Primal Hunger**.  
- Reintroduce body signals as useful data, not enemies.

---

#### 3.3 Soother

> **One-line:** uses food to blunt emotion / stress.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: medium; body cues entangled with emotion  
- Restraint: low–medium, fluctuates with mood  
- Emotional: **high**  
- External/Uncontrolled: medium–high under distress  
- Intuitive: low

**Analogue**

- High DEBQ Emotional  
- High EES  
- Often higher TFEQ Uncontrolled

**Risks**

- Over-reliance on food as primary regulatory tool.  
- Greater ED risk when combined with high restraint and low support.

**SPL focus**

- First, ensure adequate **fuelling** so hunger isn’t doing half the work.  
- Build a **non-food regulation menu** (breathwork, movement, social contact, etc.).  
- Remove moralising/shame; analyse patterns instead of self-attack.  
- De-couple the trigger from the response.  
- In severe cases, coordinate or refer for psychological support.

---

#### 3.4 Reactor

> **One-line:** high restraint + high loss of control; out-of-control episodes, often after restriction.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: medium–high, heavily self-critical  
- Restraint: high, often rigid  
- Emotional: medium–high  
- External/Uncontrolled: high (binge-like episodes)  
- Intuitive: low

**Analogue**

- High **Restraint** + high **Uncontrolled** + high **Emotional**.  
- Common in binge-type spectra.

**Risks**

- High distress, ED risk, weight cycling.  
- Strong overlap with clinical conditions in some cases.

**SPL focus**

- Always run **Reactor Triage**:

    1. Fix **Primal Hunger** (under-fuelling) first.  
    1. Dismantle **rigid rules** second.  
    1. Address **emotional soothing** last.

- Keep deficits modest; avoid extreme diets.  
- Encourage clinical support when binge intensity/frequency, distress or compensatory behaviours signal ED territory.

---

#### 3.5 Environment-Driven Eater

> **One-line:** eats whatever is around; defaults to convenience and cues.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: low–medium; internal cues drowned by environment  
- Restraint: low–medium  
- Emotional: low–medium  
- External/Uncontrolled: **high**  
- Intuitive: low

**Analogue**

- High DEBQ External  
- High TFEQ Uncontrolled  
- Overlaps with CSIRO Craver/Freewheeler/Socialiser patterns depending on context.

**Risks**

- Chronic small surpluses (grazing, snacking).  
- Misperception: “I don’t eat that much” while intake is high.

**SPL focus**

- Environment design as **primary lever**:  
    - visibility, proximity, default options, portioning  
- Install **anchor meals** to reduce grazing.  
- Add friction:  
    - no eating from packets  
    - no standing eating  
    - pause before second portions

**Neuro overlay**

- If strong impulsivity / novelty-seeking / ADHD traits:  
    - pair environment changes with **non-food dopamine** and Level 2 scaffolding.

---

#### 3.6 Ghost Eater

> **One-line:** under-eats chronically / forgets to eat; low appetite or high distraction.

**Pattern**

- Awareness: low; blunted/delayed hunger/fullness  
- Restraint: variable (busy/overloaded vs chronic dieter)  
- Emotional: low–medium  
- External/Uncontrolled: medium (overshooting when hunger finally breaks through)  
- Intuitive: low

**Analogue**

- Seen in RED-S, chronic dieters, some ADHD/ASD individuals with interoceptive deficits.

**Risks**

- In active people: RED-S, endocrine disruption, impaired bone health.  
- In neurodivergent people: long-term neglect of basic bodily needs.

**SPL focus**

- Always run **Ghost Triage**:

    1. **RED-S/low energy** → mechanical eating + medical referral  
    1. **Neurodivergent pattern** → external triggers and low-friction food  
    1. **Habit** → scheduling + gradual interoception rebuild

## APPENDIX A – CSIRO DIET TYPES → SPL ARCHETYPES

### A.1 Why this appendix exists

CSIRO’s “Diet Types” (Thinker, Craver, Foodie, Socialiser, Freewheeler) are widely publicised personality-style diet profiles built from large-scale survey data.

SPL uses a different backbone (dimensions, triage, archetypes), but many people already know their CSIRO type.

This appendix is a **translation guide**, not a second system.

### A.2 CSIRO Diet Types (compressed)

- **Thinker** – overthinks, worries about failure, high self-monitoring, stress-sensitive.  
- **Craver** – frequent strong cravings, difficult to resist tempting food, poorer diet quality.  
- **Foodie** – high enjoyment and interest in food, likes variety and experience, sometimes richer diets.  
- **Socialiser** – social context and alcohol are central, resents strict rules.  
- **Freewheeler** – impulsive, spontaneous, low planning and structure, on-the-spot eating.

### A.3 Mapping to SPL Archetypes

|CSIRO Type|Core description|Rough SPL equivalents|SPL perspective|
|--|--|--|--|
|**Thinker**|Overthinks, fears failure, high worry, stress easily derails dieting.|Mainly **Controller**, with **Reactor** mode under pressure.|High restraint, often rigid, with anxious rumination. SPL focuses on flexible restraint, error recovery, and ensuring they aren’t chronically under-fuelled.|
|**Craver**|Strong cravings, hard to resist tempting foods, often poor discretionary intake control.|Mix of **Reactor** + **Environment-Driven**, sometimes Soother.|“High drive + low containment.” SPL asks: is this Primal Hunger, rigid backlash, or loud environment? We then pick interventions accordingly.|
|**Foodie**|Loves food, variety, experience; often decent diet quality but can overdo energy-dense foods.|Either **Regulated / Intuitive** or **Environment-Driven**, depending on structure.|SPL distinguishes **Regulated Foodie** (high enjoyment + structure) from **Chaotic Foodie** (high enjoyment + no structure). We lean into pleasure but add anchors and guardrails.|
|**Socialiser**|Social eating and drinking central; hates rules that wreck social life; diet fails with people around.|Contextual **Environment-Driven** layered over base archetype (Regulated or Controller).|SPL expects context shifts: Socialisers may be fine alone but flip archetypes socially. We work on scripts, boundaries, and social defaults rather than just solo rules.|
|**Freewheeler**|Impulsive, little planning, poor overall diet quality, many on-the-spot choices.|Strongly **Environment-Driven**, possible **Ghost** traits, often ADHD-flavoured.|High external/uncontrolled eating + low structure. SPL emphasises environment design, defaults, and Level 2 scaffolding; often suggests ADHD evaluation if impairment is broad.|

### A.4 How SPL uses this

If someone arrives saying “I’m a CSIRO Craver / Thinker / Freewheeler”:

- We treat that as a **hint**, not a diagnosis.  
- We translate it to likely SPL archetype patterns.  
- Then we still run:  
    - Level 1 & 2 triage  
    - dimensional assessment  
    - proper SPL profiling

CSIRO gives you an accessible story.\
SPL converts that story into **triage + dimensions + targeted methods**.

- Lowe et al. – Power of Food Scale (PFS).

**Typologies & Clusters**

- CSIRO / Brindal et al. – Diet Types (Thinker, Craver, Foodie, Socialiser, Freewheeler).  
- Cluster studies linking high Restraint + high Uncontrolled + high Emotional eating with “unsuccessful dieters” and binge-prone groups.  
- Clusters of low restraint + low uncontrolled as more “regulated” eaters.

**Critical & Conceptual Work**

- Reviews of emotional eating scales and the gap between self-report and lab behaviour.  
- Intuitive eating systematic reviews (associations with mental health, weight stability, cardiometabolic measures).  
- RED-S consensus statements (low energy availability, hormonal suppression, appetite changes).  
- Work on interoception, mindful eating, and their relationship to eating disorders and obesity.
