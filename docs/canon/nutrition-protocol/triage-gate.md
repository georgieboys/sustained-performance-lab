The 80–20 Nutrition Protocol is not “eat clean and hope for the best.”\
It assumes three things are true before we start:

1. Your **hardware** (body) is medically safe to train.  
1. Your **operating system** (brain) is understood well enough that we don’t confuse clinical problems with “willpower.”  
1. Your **reality** (time, money, family, kitchen, culture) is on the table and not ignored.

If any of those are wrong, you don’t need a better protocol.\
You need different help.

The triage gate has four layers:

- Level 0 – Reality constraints  
- Level 1 – Physiology (Hardware)  
- Level 2 – Neurology / Mental health (Operating System)  
- Level 3 – Psychology & Behaviour (Software)

Only after triage do we apply 80–20.

---

## 1 Level 0 – Reality Constraints

Before lab values or labels, SPL asks:

- What is your actual food environment like?  
- What is your actual budget?  
- Who buys and cooks food?  
- What does your work schedule look like? Commutes? Night shifts?  
- Who do you live with and what do _they_ eat?  
- What are your non-negotiables (religious/cultural foods, family rituals, alcohol, etc.)?

This matters because:

> Any nutrition plan that ignores your constraints is fantasy fiction, not a protocol.

At this level, we’re not judging. We are:

- mapping **constraints** (time, money, skills, social obligations),  
- deciding **what’s realistically possible**,  
- deciding **how aggressive** we can be without everything collapsing.

If reality is brutal (two jobs, kids, no kitchen, low cash), the 80–20 Protocol will:

- focus on **fewer, bigger levers** (protein anchors, one or two key swaps, default meals),  
- de-prioritise complexity (no 12-ingredient recipes, no fancy timing tricks),  
- accept that “good enough” beats “perfect for three days then crash.”

Reality is not an excuse. It’s a design spec.

### 1.1 Level 1 – Physiology (Hardware)

Now we ask: _is it even safe to mess with your nutrition at all without a doctor leading?_

##### Question 1 – Is this medically unsafe or clearly not okay?

Red-flag signs (not exhaustive):

- rapid, unexplained weight loss  
- fainting, chest pain, severe breathlessness  
- persistent vomiting, blood in stool, severe abdominal pain  
- obvious severe RED-S features in active people:  
    - amenorrhoea or very irregular periods  
    - markedly suppressed libido/testosterone markers  
    - stress fractures, non-healing injuries  
- severe eating-disorder behaviours:  
    - self-induced vomiting  
    - laxative/diuretic abuse  
    - extreme restriction (< ~800 kcal/day) outside clinical care  
    - compulsive exercise with clear underweight  
- any doctor-led diet critical for survival:  
    - insulin dosing diet  
    - renal diets  
    - post-surgery nutrition, etc.

If **any** of these show up:

- SPL does **not** take the lead.  
- Medical care is non-negotiable.  
- The 80–20 Protocol is **not** your primary tool; at best, we support clinician-led care.

##### Question 2 – Is hardware stable but clearly under-optimised?

Signs:

- under-eating relative to body size and training, but no obvious endocrine catastrophe yet  
- elevated TG:HDL or other risk markers, but no acute crisis  
- fatigue, poor recovery, low mood, but not in the red-flag zone  
- GI discomfort that’s been medically checked and cleared of serious disease  
- poor sleep and high stress, but life isn’t completely collapsing

If **this** is the zone:

> Your hardware is basically intact but under-fueled and mis-used.

Here, 80–20 can legitimately:

- raise energy availability,  
- lock in protein & fibre baselines,  
- use simple carb timing if needed,  
- add basic sleep/recovery structure,  
- track agreed markers (TG:HDL, VO₂max, dead hang, resting HR, etc.).

### 1.2 Level 2 – Neurology / Mental Health (Operating System)

Now we look at the brain and mind:

- ADHD, autism spectrum, executive function  
- Major depression, anxiety, PTSD  
- Substance use  
- Medications that affect appetite, mood or weight

SPL does not diagnose or treat these. We split them into:

- **Clinical neurodivergence / mental illness** – clinical lane  
- **Functional traits** – SPL can scaffold

##### Clinical neurodivergence / mental illness (outside SPL’s core lane)

We mean:

- someone meets diagnostic criteria **and**  
- has **moderate to severe impairment** in daily life, e.g.:  
    - cannot hold basic work/school tasks together  
    - severe meltdowns/shutdowns around routine demands  
    - extreme sensory issues make eating environments impossible  
    - major depression with suicidal ideation, self-harm, severe anxiety/OCD  
- under active psychiatric/specialist care, symptoms not well controlled

In this case:

**SPL is ****_not_**** the primary intervention for food or behaviour.**

We can:

- support with simple structures,  
- make food easier and less chaotic,  
- help with environment design,

but:

- therapy/psychiatry/medical care leads,  
- the 80–20 Protocol is not used as a substitute for treatment.

##### Functional neurodivergent traits (inside SPL’s lane, with caveats)

Many people are:

- working, parenting, functioning,  
- but clearly “ADHD-ish” or “autistic-ish”:  
    - time-blindness, chronic lateness, can’t plan meals  
    - hyperfocus that nukes hunger awareness  
    - sensory picky with textures/foods  
    - overwhelmed by decision-making in supermarkets

We treat this as **functional neurodivergence**:

- wiring is different, but hardware is not in acute crisis  
- they may be undiagnosed or diagnosed-and-stable

Here, 80–20:

- accepts the wiring as fixed,  
- changes **demands and environment**, not the firmware.

Tools:

- timers, reminders, checklists  
- default meals and very low-friction options  
- realistic planning (no spreadsheet fantasy),  
- sensory-friendly food options

We **do not** promise to erase ADHD/autism.\
We design an 80–20 system that can survive them.

### 1.3 Level 3 – Psychology & Behaviour (Software)

If:

- hardware is medically safe enough,  
- operating system is understood and scaffolded,  
- reality constraints are mapped and accepted,

then we finally ask:

> “Given this body, this brain, and this life, **how do you actually eat?**”

Here we deploy the SPL behavioural machinery:

- eating-style dimensions:  
    - Awareness / interoception  
    - Cognitive Restraint (rigid vs flexible)  
    - Emotional Eating  
    - External & Uncontrolled Eating  
    - Intuitive Eating traits  
- SPL archetypes:  
    - Regulated / Intuitive  
    - Controller  
    - Soother  
    - Reactor  
    - Environment-Driven  
    - Ghost  
- environment design rules  
- emotional/behavioural tools

This is where the **80–20 playbooks for each archetype** will live later.\
But they only make sense **after** triage.

### 1.4 High-Risk Archetype Triage: Ghost & Reactor

In the 80–20 context, two patterns sit close to the “clinical” line:

- **Ghost** – chronically “not hungry,” often skipping meals, especially with training or high workload  
- **Reactor** – repeated “I lost control and ate everything” episodes, high shame

These are **never** treated as “just an archetype” without extra checks.

##### Ghost Eater Triage

1. **Metabolic Alarm (RED-S / low energy)**

    - Check: resting HR, cold extremities, fatigue, menstrual changes, libido, injury/recovery, performance.  
    - If suspicious:  
        - treat as possible RED-S  
        - mechanical eating (fixed meals regardless of hunger)  
        - reduce training if needed  
        - **medical referral**

1. **Neurodivergence**

    - Hyperfocus, object permanence issues, sensory aversions, clear ADHD/ASD traits?  
    - If yes:  
        - treat as wiring  
        - use external triggers and visible, ready-to-eat food  
        - suggest formal assessment if impairment is wide

1. **Behavioural Ghost**

    - Only if 1 and 2 are clear:  
        - schedule meals as appointments  
        - attach them to existing routines (coffee, commute, meetings)  
        - rebuild awareness with simple hunger/fullness check-ins

##### Reactor Triage

1. **Primal Hunger (under-fuelling)**

    - Skipped meals? Very low calories? Hard training and little food?  
    - If yes:  
        - increase calories earlier in the day  
        - anchor protein in meals  
        - add carbs before usual binge window

1. **Rigid Restraint / “Halo” Problem**

    - Food moralism? “One slip, day is ruined”? Hard “never” rules?  
    - If yes:  
        - dismantle rigid rules  
        - reintroduce “forbidden” foods in moderate portions  
        - explicitly practice slip-and-recover, not slip-and-spiral

1. **Emotional Soothing**

    - Only if fuelling and rules are sane:  
        - map emotional triggers  
        - build non-food regulation options  
        - if episodes are severe, frequent, or include compensatory behaviours → ED/clinical pathway, not SPL-only.

---

### 1.5 Gate Outcome: Who the 80–20 Protocol Is For

After triage, the 80–20 Nutrition Protocol is for people who:

- do **not** have red-flag hardware issues, or are medically cleared and stable,  
- do **not** have uncontrolled or severe clinical neuropsychiatric illness driving eating,  
- may have functional ADHD/ASD traits, but are broadly independent,  
- are willing to be honest about their reality constraints,  
- and want a **simple, robust baseline nutrition system** tuned to their wiring and life.

Everyone else:

- needs medical and/or clinical mental-health care first,  
- can still use **pieces** of SPL (checklists, environment tweaks),  
- but should **not** treat the 80–20 Protocol as a primary treatment for disease.
