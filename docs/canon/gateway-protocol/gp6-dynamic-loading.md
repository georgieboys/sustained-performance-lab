A ballistic hip-hinge system for “honest power”, built on the dead-stop kettlebell swing.

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

Dynamic Loading is the “moving weight” block of SPL.\
If Isometrics give you unshakeable positions, Dynamic Loading teaches you to move load through space without leaking energy or losing shape.

The dead-stop kettlebell swing is a **high-value hinge power drill** when nested inside a broader system that respects progression, trunk control, and joint history. Dynamic Loading is therefore placed **after structural blocks** and **before more chaotic conditioning**, giving the engine a robust, well-patterned way to express force.

For now, we standardise **one canonical movement**:

- **Dead-stop kettlebell swing** — a ballistic hip hinge where every rep starts from a dead stop on the floor or between the feet. No cycling, no bouncing, no “flappy” swings. Each rep is its own clean, precise event.

**Canonical protocol (SPL base block):**

- **20 reps → 2–3 min rest → repeat 4 rounds**

This block sits between slow structural work (Isometrics, Knee, Low Back) and denser conditioning work (VO₂ protocols, complexes). It’s the bridge from **“can hold positions”** to **“can express power safely.”**

---

## 2. PURPOSE

The Dynamic Loading (Kettlebell First) block is designed to:

- Build **hip power** through fast, explosive hip extension.  
- Train **bracing at speed** so ribs–spine–pelvis stay organised while the bell moves.  
- Develop **rhythm** and timing between breath, hips, and kettlebell.  
- Increase **hinge confidence** so loaded hip bending feels natural instead of threatening.  
- Provide a clean **transition into conditioning**, giving exposure to higher heart rates and repeated efforts without the chaos of full circuits yet.

Dynamic Loading is **not** meant to be a full standalone strength program.\
It is a **high-leverage connector** that teaches the body to apply force quickly through a stable frame, so later conditioning and sport don’t dismantle you.

---

## 3. BENEFITS

Properly progressed dead-stop swings provide:

**Strength & power**

- Posterior chain strength across **glutes, hamstrings, and spinal erectors**.  
- **Rate-of-force development (RFD):** ability to go from “off” to “on” quickly — relevant for slips, jumps, changes of direction, and “catch yourself” moments.

**Patterning & durability**

- Repeated, precise hinges at manageable loads build confidence in the “bend and pick up” shape.  
- Angles and bracing patterns map onto deadlifts, everyday lifting, and many sport tasks.

**Trunk stiffness & energy transfer**

- The trunk learns to be a quiet bridge: hips generate power, arms steer, spine stays boring and predictable.  
- Better trunk stiffness often translates into more stable running, jumping, and lifting.

**Low-impact conditioning bridge**

- Heart rate rises and you get a clear conditioning effect without high-impact pounding.  
- Simple sets and rests are easy to dose, scale, and plug into later SPL blocks.

## 4. GLOBAL EXECUTION PRINCIPLES

### 4.1 Exercise Levels (Per Movement)

The kettlebell hinge family has four technical levels:

- **Level 1 — Patterning**\
No swing yet. Learn the hinge and basic deadlift pattern.  
- **Level 2 — Competent Swing (Light)**\
Full dead-stop swings with a light/moderate bell and crisp positions.  
- **Level 3 — Advanced Swing (Power / Density)**\
Heavier bells or tighter rest intervals, while preserving technique.  
- **Level 4 — Complex / Loaded Variants**\
Heavier bells, power focus, or integration into larger circuits once the base block is automatic.

Levels are per person, not per ego.\
Being Level 3 in deadlift but Level 1–2 in swings is feedback, not failure.

---

### 4.2 Session Structure (How You Actually Do This)

Dynamic Loading (Kettlebell First) is performed as repeat sets of the **same movement** rather than a multi-exercise circuit.

**Canonical block (SPL standard):**

- **4 × 20 dead-stop swings**  
- **2–3 minutes rest** between sets

**On-ramp options:**

- **Block Level 1:** 4 × 10 dead-stop swings, 2–3 min rest  
- **Block Level 2:** 4 × 15 dead-stop swings, 2–3 min rest  
- **Block Level 3 (Canonical):** 4 × 20 dead-stop swings, 2–3 min rest

Frequency: typically **2–3 sessions per week**, with at least one rest day between.

You can think of it in the same “circuit levels” language used elsewhere in SPL:

- **Circuit Level 1:** ability to complete a reduced-volume version (e.g. 2 sets).  
- **Circuit Level 2:** moderate volume (e.g. 3 sets).  
- **Circuit Level 3:** full canonical structure (4 × 20).

---

### 4.3 Load Selection & Effort

**Bell choice:**

- **Too light:** all 20 reps feel like a warm-up and hips never really have to work.  
- **Too heavy:**  
    - Form degrades before rep 15,  
    - You can’t park the bell under control, or  
    - Your back (not hips/glutes) is the limiter.

**Effort guideline:**

- Aim for **7–8/10 effort** on the last 3–5 reps.  
- You should finish breathing hard but still able to execute a clean park.  
- If the last reps are survival chaos, regress **load**, **volume**, or **level**.

SPL rule: **change one variable at a time** — volume, density, or load — not all three.

---

### 4.4 Breathing & Pain Rules

**Breathing:**

- Small preparatory inhale before the hike, gentle brace.  
- Exhale as the bell comes up or at the top; keep ribcage stacked over pelvis.  
- Avoid long, continuous Valsalva across high-rep sets unless cleared and experienced.

**Pain:**

- Muscle fatigue in glutes, hamstrings, and grip = expected.  
- **Not acceptable:**  
    - Sharp or localised spine, hip, or knee pain.  
    - Zapping, radiating, or “nerve-ish” sensations.  
    - Feeling like individual spinal segments are being pinched or jammed.

**SPL rule:** end the set when **position fails**, not when **pride fails**.

---

### 4.5 Progression Logic

Progress this block when:

- You can complete **4 × 20** dead-stop swings at a given load with stable technique.  
- The next day your hips and back feel **“used but fine”** (mild muscular stiffness ok; joint pain not ok).

Then adjust:

- **Volume**  
    - Start at 4 × 10 → 4 × 15 → 4 × 20.  
- **Density**  
    - Once 4 × 20 is solid at 2–3 min rest, gradually bring rest down toward **90–120 seconds**.  
- **Load**  
    - When 4 × 20 at ~2 min rest feels routine and crisp, move up one bell size and reset rest to **2–3 minutes**.

The system assumes long-term iteration: **fill time at a given level → then make small, controlled upgrades.**

## 5. EXERCISE 1 — DEAD-STOP KETTLEBELL SWING

### 5.1 Purpose

- Train **explosive hip extension** with a stable trunk.  
- Teach the body to accept and redirect force through the hinge pattern.  
- Build **grip** and shoulder tolerance in a non-overhead, dynamic pattern.  
- Prepare for more complex kettlebell and barbell work later in the Canon.

---

### 5.2 Standard Setup

**Start position**

- Bell on the floor slightly in front, handle roughly aligned with mid-foot.  
- Feet about hip-width, toes turned out slightly as comfortable.  
- Soften knees, then **hinge**: hips travel back, shins nearly vertical, chest over bell.  
- Grab handle with both hands, thumbs around, shoulders packed (down and slightly back).  
- Spine neutral: no rounding, no forced arch.

**Hike**

- Tip the bell slightly toward you.  
- Take a small preparatory breath and **hike** the bell back between your thighs like a hard football snap.  
- Upper arms close to ribs, forearms between thighs, bell path tight to the body.

**Swing**

- From the deepest point of the hike, **snap hips forward**:  
    - Hips and knees extend together.  
    - Glutes contract hard; quads finish the knee extension.  
    - The bell **floats** to about chest height from hip power, not arm lifting.  
- At the top:  
    - Hips and knees extended.  
    - Ribs stacked over pelvis (no backward lean).  
    - Arms relaxed but straight, bell hovering at chest height.

**Park (dead stop)**

- Let the bell swing back down as you **re-hinge**: hips back, shins nearly vertical.  
- Guide the bell along the same tight arc back to the floor in front of you.  
- Bell stops completely.  
- Reset stance, breath, and brace before the next rep.

**Key standards**

- Every rep starts from a **static start** — no rebounding or continuous cycling.  
- Hips travel back more than knees bend; if it looks like a squat, it is not a swing.  
- Spine stays neutral top and bottom; no collapse, no hyperextension.  
- Bell path is compact and predictable; it never drifts far away from the body.

---

### 5.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Patterning (No Swing Yet)**

Goal: learn the hinge and simple loaded pattern.

- **Kettlebell Romanian deadlift / deadlift**  
    - Same stance and hinge as above.  
    - Lift bell from floor or from just below knees with control.  
    - Focus on “hips back, hips through” with ribs stacked over pelvis.

Use **3–4 × 8–12** RDLs until the hinge shape feels automatic and the back feels safe.

---

**Level 2 — Competent Dead-Stop Swing (Light Bell)**

Goal: own the basic swing pattern with a manageable load.

- Choose a light to moderate bell.  
- Perform **4 × 10–15** dead-stop swings.  
- Park the bell every rep.  
- Focus on consistent hike, strong but not wild hip snap, and quiet spine.

Progress toward the canonical **4 × 20** when **4 × 15** feels clean and repeatable.

---

**Level 3 — Advanced Swing (Power / Density)**

Goal: increase output while keeping the pattern stable.

Options:

- **Heavier bell** with the same basic structure: work **4 × 10 → 4 × 20** at 2–3 min rest.  
- **Same bell, denser work:** keep **4 × 20** and gradually reduce rest toward **90–120 seconds**.

Once base is stable, occasional **power-focused sets** of 5–10 maximal-quality reps may be used with longer rests.

---

**Level 4 — Complex / Integrated Variants**

Used only after Level 3 is automatic and joints are consistently happy.

Examples:

- Heavier bells for short sets (e.g. **6–10 reps**) with long rest.  
- Swings integrated into larger SPL conditioning circuits (**swings → carries → isometrics**).  
- More advanced power work (e.g. ladders) for specific athletic contexts.

For general populations, **Level 3 is usually sufficient**; Level 4 is optional and context-dependent.

## 6. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION — SUMMARY

The Dynamic Loading (Kettlebell First) block sits on the following principles:

**Hip hinge and posterior chain development**

- Hinge-based kettlebell swings load **glutes, hamstrings, and spinal erectors** in a pattern similar to many real-world lifts and bends, sharing load across the chain rather than isolating a single segment.

**Power & rate-of-force development (RFD)**

- Ballistic hip extension drills are a practical, accessible way to train **RFD** in non-elite populations, especially when Olympic lifts are too technical or high-risk for a given individual or setting.

**Spinal loading with organised trunk**

- Swings produce meaningful but tolerable spinal forces when performed with a **neutral spine** and solid bracing. In the context of prior isometric and low-back work, this can enhance resilience rather than harm.

**Conditioning with low impact**

- Properly programmed swings increase heart rate and provide a cardiovascular stimulus with relatively low impact, useful for people who cannot tolerate large volumes of running or jumping.
