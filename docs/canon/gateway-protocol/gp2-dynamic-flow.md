_A ten-minute continuous movement sequence for “switching the system on.”_

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

Dynamic Flow is the active warm-up block of SPL. It is ten minutes of uninterrupted, rhythmic movement designed to shift the body from “idle” to “ready.” No stretching, no holds, no chasing range, no intensity. The goal is simple: loosen the major joints, raise body temperature, and wake up natural movement patterns.

The sequence is built around nine simple drills:

- Rebound Hops — whole-body rhythm and gentle impact.  
- Body Waves — spine articulation from hips to head.  
- One-Arm Swings — loose pendulum motion for the shoulders.  
- Trunk Twists — rotational freedom through the mid-section.  
- Golf Swings — wide, elastic rotational swings.  
- Dead Arms — passive swinging to relax the upper body.  
- Marches with Slaps — contralateral rhythm and coordination.  
- Hip Circles — smooth hip joint motion.  
- Side Bends — lateral flexion and rib mobility.

These movements represent how humans actually move through the world: bouncing, rotating, swaying, stepping, and bending. The aim is lightness, looseness, and rhythmic ease — not effort.

---

## 2. PURPOSE

The Dynamic Flow block is designed to:

- Raise body temperature and circulation safely.  
- Prepare muscles and joints for isometrics, swings, and later loading.  
- Restore basic movement patterns lost through sitting and daily stiffness.  
- Improve proprioception (body awareness) through gentle, multi-joint motion.  
- Activate rotation, lateral motion, and contralateral coordination without strain.

Dynamic Flow is not mobility work or cardio. It is the transition layer that makes the rest of the session safer, smoother, and more efficient.

---

## 3. BENEFITS

#### Movement readiness

Light, rhythmic motion increases tissue temperature and improves ease of movement. This reduces early-session stiffness and makes strength work cleaner.

### Joint comfort

The sequence uses natural arcs and swings that circulate synovial fluid through the hips, shoulders, spine, and knees — improving joint lubrication and comfort.

### Neuromuscular priming

Cross-body marching, twisting, and swinging sharpen coordination and balance. These patterns wake up the nervous system without fatigue.

### Multi-plane movement

Dynamic Flow moves the body through sagittal, frontal, and transverse planes. Training across planes reduces compensations during isometrics and swings.

### Low-risk, high-reward preparation

No end-range stretching. No holds. No forced positions.\
Just natural human motion that prepares the system without taxing it.

---

## 4. GLOBAL EXECUTION PRINCIPLES

### 4.1 Structure (How You Actually Do This)

Dynamic Flow is one continuous block:

- Duration: ~10 minutes  
- Drills: 9 total, performed in order  
- Tempo: relaxed and rhythmic  
- Rest: none  
- Breathing: natural, not effortful

Move from one drill to the next without pause. The goal is an unbroken flow of loose, easy movement.

### 4.2 Timing

Most drills last **30–60 seconds**, with fixed one-minute drills noted.\
Combined, the full sequence naturally lands around ten minutes.

### 4.3 Effort

Effort stays around **3–4/10**.\
You should feel warmer and looser by the end — not tired.

### 4.4 Pain Rules

Light stiffness = fine.\
Joint pain, pinching, sharp sensations, or “this feels wrong” = stop and adjust.\
Dynamic Flow should feel comfortable and natural.

---

## 5. THE SEQUENCE — MOVEMENT BY MOVEMENT

### 5.1 Rebound Hops (30–60s)

Light bouncing in place with loose arms. Let the body shake and find a rhythm.

### 5.2 Body Waves (30–60s)

A slow wave from hips to head. Move smoothly without forcing depth or speed.

### 5.3 One-Arm Swings (30s per side)

Swing one arm forward and back like a loose pendulum. Switch sides after 30 seconds.

### 5.4 Trunk Twists (1 min)

Hands lightly together in front. Rotate left and right in a relaxed, consistent rhythm.

### 5.5 Golf Swings (30s per side)

Wide, elastic rotational swings — like following through after a strike. Let the arms travel. Switch sides.

### 5.6 Dead Arms (1 min)

Arms hanging loose. Swing them freely without controlling the motion. Shoulders stay soft.

### 5.7 Marches with Slaps (1 min)

March in place. As each knee lifts, gently slap the opposite hand across the body.

### 5.8 Hip Circles (30s per side)

Shift onto one leg and draw slow circles with the free leg. Switch after 30 seconds.

### 5.9 Side Bends (30–60s)

Lean side-to-side with smooth arcs. No reaching for range.

## 6. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION — SUMMARY

### Temperature & circulation

Rhythmic, low-load movement increases tissue temperature and blood flow. Warm tissues move more smoothly, produce force more effectively, and exhibit reduced early-session injury risk.

### Joint lubrication

Gentle arcs and swings circulate synovial fluid through major joints. This improves comfort and reduces friction at the start of a session.

### Proprioception & coordination

Movement patterns like twisting, marching, and swinging increase proprioceptive input — the nervous system’s awareness of where the body is in space.

### Multi-plane movement

Dynamic Flow covers sagittal (hops, marches), transverse (twists, golf swings), and frontal (side bends) planes. Multi-plane warm-ups are more effective at reducing compensatory patterns than single-plane work.

### Dynamic vs static warm-ups

Evidence consistently supports dynamic (moving) warm-ups before strength training. Static stretching can temporarily reduce strength and power output; Dynamic Flow avoids all static holds.

### Contralateral patterning

Marches with slaps activate the left-right coordination patterns essential for walking and running. This improves timing and nervous-system readiness before isometrics or swings.
