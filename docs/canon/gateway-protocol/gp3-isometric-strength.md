_A static-strength system for “unshakeable positions”, run as a circuit._

---

## 1. OVERVIEW

Isometrics means **holding precise positions under load**. No reps, no bouncing, no momentum. You assume the position, you hold it, you breathe, you repeat.

Isometrics are **keystone infrastructure** — they help your frame meet the rest of the protocol without complaining.

We standardise five core positions:

1. **Dead Hang** — shoulders, grip, and spinal traction.  
1. **Front Plank** — global trunk brace (ribs–spine–pelvis).  
1. **Wall Sit** — bilateral knee and quad tolerance.  
1. **90-90 Lunge Hold** — single-leg control, hip–pelvis–knee alignment.  
1. **Glute Bridge Hold** — posterior chain hip extension (glutes, hamstrings).

These map onto how humans actually live: hanging, bracing, sitting into the world, stepping, and driving the hips — and give angle-specific strength that complements dynamic lifting.

---

## 2. PURPOSE

The Isometric Block is designed to:

- Build **baseline structural strength** across shoulders, trunk, hips, and knees.  
- Increase **tolerance of key joint angles** used in daily life and later SPL work.  
- Improve **joint position awareness** (“where am I in space?”) under load.  
- Provide a **low-impact strength option** for people who are:  
    - deconditioned or new to strength training,  
    - returning from time off, or  
    - temporarily unable to tolerate high-velocity or high-impact work.

Isometrics are not a full replacement for dynamic resistance training. They are a **high-leverage base layer** that makes everything else safer, cleaner, and more productive.

---

## 3. BENEFITS

Isometrics are a **powerful, evidence-supported tool** for strength, tendon load tolerance, and BP modulation — when programmed progressively and sanely.

### Strength & angle specificity

- Isometric strength training reliably increases maximal strength, especially at and around the trained joint angle, when effort is high and enough time under tension is accumulated.

### Transfer & performance

- Training at multiple joint angles and combining isometrics with dynamic work improves transfer to dynamic performance (running, jumping, etc.).

### Tendon & connective tissue

- Chronic mechanical loading (including heavy isometrics) can increase tendon stiffness, modulus, and sometimes cross-sectional area, especially in lower-limb tendons.

### Tendinopathy pain

- Heavy isometric bouts can produce short-term pain relief and increased force output in patellar tendinopathy (Rio et al.), though later work shows mixed results and tendon-specific variability.  
- SPL stance: useful tool, not a magic eraser — belongs inside broader loading and rehab plans.

### Blood pressure

- Multiple meta-analyses and recent reviews show **isometric exercise training** (IET) — particularly wall squats and handgrip — can be one of the most effective exercise modes for lowering resting blood pressure in adults with elevated BP.  
- However, **acute blood pressure spikes during heavy isometric holds can be large**, especially with breath-holding; people with hypertension or cardiovascular disease need tailored protocols and medical oversight.

## 4. GLOBAL EXECUTION PRINCIPLES

### 4.1 Exercise Levels (Per Hold)

Each of the five holds has four **technical levels**:

- **Level 1 — Beginner**\
Regressed angles, more support, shorter holds. Learn the position.  
- **Level 2 — Competent**\
Standard angles, consistent holds. Own the position.  
- **Level 3 — Advanced**\
Longer holds and/or more complexity at similar or deeper angles.  
- **Level 4 — Weighted**\
External load added while preserving Level-2/3 quality.

Levels are **per exercise**. You might be Level 3 in glute bridge, Level 1 in dead hang. That gap is not failure; it’s a map.

---

### 4.2 Circuit Structure (How You Actually Do This)

The Isometric Block is performed as a **circuit**:

> **One circuit = one set of each of the 5 holds**\
> Dead Hang → Plank → Wall Sit → 90-90 → Glute Bridge

- Move through the five positions in order.  
- Rest **15–45 seconds between holds** as needed.  
- After all 5, rest **1–2 minutes**, then repeat the circuit.

**Circuit volume levels:**

- **Circuit Level 1:** 1 full circuit of Level-1 versions of all five exercises.  
    - This is a solid starting point for beginners.  
- **Circuit Level 2:** 2 full circuits of Level-1 versions.  
- **Circuit Level 3 (SPL Base Competence):**  
    - Ability to complete **3 circuits** using **Level-1 variants** of all five holds, with clean form and controlled breathing.

From there, progression typically looks like:

1. Lock in **3 circuits at Level-1** holds → SPL base frame online.  
1. Begin upgrading individual holds to **Level-2** variations while maintaining **2–3 circuits**.  
1. Over time, aim for **3 circuits with mostly Level-2** holds before flirting with Level-3/4 and heavier work.

---

### 4.3 Hold Duration & Effort (Per Position inside a Circuit)

For each hold _inside_ the circuit:

- **Level 1:** 10–20 seconds  
- **Level 2:** 20–30 seconds  
- **Level 3:** 30–45 seconds  
- **Level 4:** 20–30 seconds (with added load)

Across 1–3 circuits, this keeps total time under tension per session in a range similar to what’s used in many isometric strength and BP-lowering protocols (≈60–180 seconds per muscle group per session).

**Effort guideline:**\
Aim for **7–8/10 effort** on most sets. You should be clearly working by the end of the hold, maybe shaking a bit, but still able to breathe and speak a short sentence.

---

### 4.4 Breathing & Pain Rules

- **Breathing:**  
    - No deliberate breath-holding.  
    - In through the nose, out through the mouth, steady rhythm.  
    - Avoid hard Valsalva straining, especially if you have blood pressure issues.  
- **Pain:**  
    - Muscle burn = fine.  
    - Joint pain, sharp pain, pins-and-needles, or “this feels wrong” = stop and regress.

SPL rule: **End the set when position fails, not when pride fails.**

---

### 4.5 Progression Logic

You progress a hold when:

1. You can hit the **top of the time range** at your current technical level (e.g. 30s Level-2 plank) **within 3 circuits**, and  
1. The next day, your joints feel “used but fine” (mild stiffness ok; significant pain not ok).

Then:

1. **Fill time** at that level →  
1. **Increase angle / complexity** →  
1. **Add load** (Level-4) only when time + angle are stable.

## 5. EXERCISE 1 — DEAD HANG

### 5.1 Purpose

- Build **grip and forearm strength**.  
- Load shoulders in an overhead pattern in a controlled way.  
- Provide mild **traction** through the spine and shoulder girdle for some people when done comfortably.

### 5.2 Standard Setup

- Stable bar, hands just outside shoulder width, thumbs wrapped.  
- Step or jump so feet are off the floor (or lightly resting for support).  
- Legs relaxed; ribs down; mild trunk brace.  
- Shoulders elevate slightly but are not forced into pain.  
- Neck neutral; gaze at the horizon.

### 5.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Beginner (Supported / Partial Hang)**

- Bar low enough that feet stay on floor or a box.  
- Use legs to off-load part of your bodyweight.  
- Option: small scapular shrugs with straight elbows.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 10–20s.

**Level 2 — Competent (Full Dead Hang)**

- Feet fully off the floor.  
- Light hollow body: ribs tucked, glutes gently on.  
- No swinging or kipping.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s.

**Level 3 — Advanced (Active / Complex Hangs)**\
Choose one:

- **Active hang:** slight scapular depression (shoulders “pulled down”).  
- **Hollow hang:** stronger hollow body, legs slightly in front.  
- **Bias hang:** shift more load to one arm without twisting (both hands still on bar).

**Hold inside circuit:** 30–45s (can be split across rounds if needed).

**Level 4 — Weighted Hang**

- Add load via dip belt or dumbbell between feet.  
- Keep Level-2/3 shape; start at **20–30s**.  
- If grip fails way before everything else, log as grip-limited and adjust.

## 6. EXERCISE 2 — FRONT PLANK

### 6.1 Purpose

- Train **anterior trunk** to resist extension (arching).  
- Stabilise rib–pelvis relationship under load.  
- Prepare for pressing, carries, and dynamic hip hinging.

### 6.2 Standard Setup (Forearm Plank)

- Elbows under shoulders, forearms parallel or forming a triangle.  
- Feet hip-width, heels driving back.  
- Squeeze glutes, slight posterior pelvic tilt, ribs down.  
- Head in line with spine.  
- Straight line from ears → shoulders → hips → ankles.

### 6.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Beginner (Knee / Incline Plank)**

- **Knee plank:** knees on floor, hips extended (no tabletop).  
- **Incline plank:** forearms on bench/table, feet on floor.

Same rules: straight-line trunk, ribs stacked over pelvis.\
**Hold inside circuit:** 10–20s.

**Level 2 — Competent (Standard Forearm Plank)**

- Full plank on forearms and toes.  
- No sagging or piking.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s.

**Level 3 — Advanced (Higher-Demand Variants)**

Pick one:

- **Long-lever plank:** elbows slightly forward of shoulders.  
- **Plank + controlled shoulder taps:** trunk stays stable as you briefly support on one arm.  
- **High-tension plank:** actively “drag” elbows toward toes without moving.

**Hold inside circuit:** 30–45s.

**Level 4 — Weighted Plank**

- Plate or sandbag across upper back.  
- Maintain Level-2 body line; start at **20–30s**.  
- Lumbar sag or rib flare = set over.

## 7. EXERCISE 3 — WALL SIT

### 7.1 Purpose

- Load **quadriceps and knees** in a simple bilateral stance.  
- Build tolerance for squats, stairs, and sit-to-stand tasks.  
- Acts as a good anchor position for BP-oriented wall-squat protocols (in clinical contexts).

### 7.2 Standard Setup (90° Wall Sit)

- Back against wall; feet ≈0.5–0.7 m in front (height dependent).  
- Slide down until hips and knees ≈90°, thighs parallel to floor.  
- Knees over mid-foot; don’t cave inward.  
- Head, shoulders, low back lightly on wall; ribs not flaring.  
- Hands off thighs.

### 7.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Beginner (High Wall Sit)**

- Stay higher: knees **>90°** (thighs sloping downward).  
- Prioritise pain-free alignment.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 10–20s.

**Level 2 — Competent (Parallel Wall Sit)**

- Hips/knees ≈90°, shins near vertical.  
- Weight through mid-foot/heel.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s.

**Level 3 — Advanced (Extended / Complex Holds)**

Choose one:

- **Extended hold:** 30–45s.  
- **Arms-overhead wall sit.**  
- **Single-leg bias:** both feet down, but more load on one leg; hips level.

**Hold inside circuit:** 30–45s.

**Level 4 — Weighted Wall Sit**

- Dumbbell / plate at chest or on thighs.  
- Restart at **20–30s** holds.  
- If load triggers knee pain, adjust angle or weight.

## 8. EXERCISE 4 — 90-90 LUNGE HOLD (SPLIT-SQUAT ISO)

### 8.1 Purpose

- Build **single-leg strength and control**.  
- Train hip–knee–ankle alignment in a walking/running-relevant stance.  
- Prepare for step-ups, lunges, and knee-dominant work.

### 8.2 Standard Setup (90-90)

- Stand tall, step forward into lunge stance.  
- Drop straight down until:  
    - Front knee ≈90°, over mid-foot.  
    - Back knee ≈90°, hovering just off floor.  
- Torso upright; ribs stacked over pelvis.  
- Hips square; front heel heavy; back foot on toes.

### 8.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Beginner (High Split Hold / Assisted)**

- Stay higher so front knee is **>90°**; back knee further from floor.  
- Use light support from wall/chair/stick if needed.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 10–20s per side.

**Level 2 — Competent (Standard 90-90)**

- Both knees ≈90°, back knee hovering.  
- Hips level; no twisting or drifting.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s per side.

**Level 3 — Advanced (Long / Challenging Holds)**

Pick one:

- **Extended holds:** 30–45s per side.  
- **Hands-overhead 90-90.**  
- **Front-heel elevation** (small plate/board under front heel).

**Hold inside circuit:** 30–45s per side.

**Level 4 — Weighted 90-90 Hold**

- Dumbbells at sides or goblet hold.  
- Maintain Level-2 angles.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** start at 20–30s per side.  
- Knee collapse or repeated back-knee contact with floor = regress.

## 9. EXERCISE 5 — GLUTE BRIDGE HOLD

### 9.1 Purpose

- Train **hip extension** (glutes + hamstrings) in a low-risk position.  
- Counterbalance prolonged sitting by loading the posterior chain.  
- Prepare for loaded hip hinging (swings, deadlifts, hip thrusts).

### 9.2 Standard Setup (Double-Leg Bridge)

- Lie on back, knees bent ≈90°, feet hip-width.  
- Arms at ~45° from torso on floor.  
- Press through heels, squeeze glutes, lift hips until shoulders → hips → knees align.  
- Ribs down; no excessive lumbar arch.  
- Neck relaxed, gaze at ceiling.

### 9.3 Levels & Progressions

**Level 1 — Beginner (Short-Lever / Partial Bridge)**

- Feet slightly closer to hips; lift hips only to comfortable height.  
- Option: small pad under sacrum to help feel hip extension line.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 10–20s.

**Level 2 — Competent (Full Bridge)**

- Full hip extension; straight line shoulders → hips → knees.  
- Heels under or just behind knees.  
- Strong glute squeeze without hamstring cramp.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s.

**Level 3 — Advanced (Single-Leg & Marching)**

Options:

- **Single-leg bridge:** extend one leg while holding hips level.  
- **Marching bridge:** alternate lifting each foot slightly while maintaining bridge.  
- **Extended holds:** 30–45s (per side for single-leg).

**Hold inside circuit:** 30–45s.

**Level 4 — Weighted Bridge Hold**

- Padded barbell/sandbag/plate across pelvis.  
- Hold at top with neutral ribs, no lumbar over-extension.  
- **Hold inside circuit:** 20–30s.  
- If tension is mainly in lower back, lighten load or regress.

## 10. SCIENTIFIC VALIDATION — SUMMARY

The SPL Isometric Block is built to sit on solid ground:

- **Strength & joint-angle adaptation**\
Isometric RT increases maximal strength, particularly at trained joint angles, with robust evidence for angle specificity and time-dependent strength gains.  
- **Transfer & performance**\
Isometrics contribute to dynamic performance when combined with dynamic RT, with clear task-specific patterns.  
- **Tendon adaptation**\
Chronic mechanical loading — including heavy isometric components — increases tendon stiffness, modulus, and sometimes CSA, especially in lower limb tendons.  
- **Tendinopathy pain**\
Isometrics can produce acute analgesia and increased force output in patellar tendinopathy, though later studies highlight heterogeneity and tendon-specific differences. SPL therefore treats isometrics as a valuable, but not universal, analgesic tool.  
- **Blood pressure**\
Meta-analyses and large trials increasingly show isometric training (handgrip, wall squat, plank-style holds) as a top performer for resting BP reduction when programmed at appropriate intensity, duration, and frequency. Acute BP spikes remain a safety consideration, especially with breath-holding, so protocols must be scaled for individual cardiovascular risk.
