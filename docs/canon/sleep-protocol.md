### RHR, Forgiveness, and Just Enough Structure to Keep You Alive

---

## 1. What This Is (and Who It’s For)

This protocol is for people whose lives look like:

- Singapore / big-city chaos  
- Kids, parents, meetings, late messages  
- Not much spare bandwidth for “perfect sleep hygiene”

You already know sleep is important. The goal here is _not_ to turn you into a quantified monk. The goal is:

- **One simple physiological signal** you can pay attention to (resting heart rate, RHR)  
- **A few cheap levers** you can pull most nights  
- **A forgiveness-first frame**, so this doesn’t become another thing to fail at

You do **not** need to do the rest of SPL to use this. This is a standalone pillar.

---

## 2. Design Principles

1. **Forgiveness-first**

    - We assume you will:  
        - Scroll your phone  
        - Miss steps  
        - Have rough weeks  
    - The protocol only ever asks:

> “What is the smallest helpful thing I can do _tonight_?”

1. **One main metric, not a dashboard**

    - We use **resting heart rate (RHR)** as a _rough_ “how loaded is my system?” signal.  
    - It’s cheap, widely measurable, and reasonably tied to arousal and sleep debt.

1. **External → Internal**

    - First: environment (light, cooling, blanket).  
    - Then: body chemistry (shower, magnesium, creatine).  
    - Then: mind (breathwork, brain dump, somatic, creative, book-not-phone).

1. **Anti-orthosomnia**

    - If tracking numbers makes you more anxious, you are explicitly allowed to drop **all** tracking and run the behaviour only.  
    - The levers work without data.

---

## 3. Scope, Safety, and When to See a Human Doctor

This is a **behavioural** protocol, not a medical treatment.

You should **not** rely on it alone (and should talk to a clinician) if:

- You may have **sleep apnea** (loud snoring, gasping, choking, witnessed apneas)  
- You have **severe insomnia** (>3 months, despite reasonable effort)  
- You have major **mental health conditions** (severe depression, mania, psychosis, PTSD nightmares)  
- You have known **heart disease, arrhythmias, POTS, or are on cardiac/autonomic meds** and your night HR is weird or worrying  
- You wake with chest pain, breathlessness, or fainting

In those cases:\
Use this protocol as a **supporting routine**, not your main plan.

## 4. The Metric: Resting Heart Rate (RHR) as a Gentle Signal

### 4.1 Why RHR?

Higher resting heart rate is consistently associated with:

- Higher sympathetic (“fight/flight”) drive  
- Higher risk of cardiovascular and metabolic disease over time  
- Worse sleep and more nighttime arousal when it’s elevated at night ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35286195/) )

In insomnia and hyperarousal states, heart rate stays higher and doesn’t drop as smoothly as people drift toward sleep; the “slope” of HR drop around lights-out is flatter. That’s a pretty decent real-world marker that your system is still wound up.

So we use RHR as:

> “How loaded is my system right now?”\
> **Not** “Am I good or bad as a person?”

### 4.2 RHR is Multi-Factor

RHR is affected by:

- Sleep debt  
- Caffeine, alcohol, late meals  
- Illness, inflammation, pain  
- Stress, anxiety, arguments  
- Meds, cycle phase, training load, hydration

So:

- A high value ≈ “system is carrying more load than usual,”  
- It does **not** cleanly tell you _why_.

We always interpret it with context, not doom.

### 4.3 People for Whom RHR is Noisy

If you have:

- Arrhythmias  
- POTS or dysautonomia  
- Are on beta-blockers or other HR-modifying meds  
- Or your device readings are all over the place

…then RHR may not be a clean signal.

You can still:

- Run **all** the behavioural pieces  
- Use **subjective** cues instead of numbers: muscle tension, breath speed, mental “buzziness”

Numbers are optional. Levers are not.

## 5. Calibration: Finding Your Floor Without Obsessing

We use mornings **only** to find your baseline. No daily micromanagement.

### 5.1 Floor Week (Once in a While)

Once at the start (then maybe every 3–4 months, or after big life shifts):

1. Pick **5 mornings** in a week.  
1. Each chosen morning:

    - Wake up  
    - Stay lying/sitting quietly  
    - **Before** coffee, phone, or walking around:  
        - Check HR on your watch / band / oximeter, **or**  
        - Count pulse for 30 seconds ×2

You get 5 numbers, e.g. 62, 60, 58, 59, 61.

Now:

- Ignore days when you were obviously sick or up half the night.  
- Take the **lowest 3 “normal-ish”** values and average them.

Example:

- Lowest normal-ish: 58, 59, 60 → floor **F ≈ 59 bpm**

If your wearable’s “resting HR” average is similar, you can just use that.

### 5.2 Frequency

- Re-run a Floor Week roughly **once a quarter**, or  
    - After major changes (training load, big weight change, long illness, new baby / shift work)

The rest of the time, we don’t care about morning HR.

## 6. Anti-Orthosomnia Clause

If any of this happens:

- You’re lying in bed watching your HR number in panic  
- You feel worse about sleep because your HR or zones aren’t “perfect”  
- You’re starting to treat this like an exam

Then the rule is:

> **Drop the numbers.**\
> Keep the cave, shower, breathing, writing, movement, book, and forgiveness.\
> You are still 100% “on protocol.”

## 7. Nightly Snapshot (Optional) and Zones

This part is **optional**. It’s there for people who like having a gauge.

### 7.1 How to Take a Nightly Snapshot

Once the cave is roughly set (see next sections):

1. Sit or lie quietly in your sleep environment for 1–2 minutes.  
1. Take a **pre-sleep HR** reading:

    - Watch / band / oximeter, or  
    - Pulse 30 seconds ×2

Call that **H₀**.

Compute: Δ = H₀ − F (how many beats above your floor).

### 7.2 Heuristic Zones (Not Exams)

We use Δ to loosely label the night:

- **Green**: Δ ≈ 0–5 bpm → “pretty calm”  
- **Amber**: Δ ≈ 6–12 bpm → “carrying some load”  
- **Red**: Δ ≥ 13 bpm → “system is really loaded”

These are **rough** ranges, not a diagnosis.

If you don’t like numbers, skip the math. Just ask:

- “Do I feel: mostly fine / a bit wired / absolutely cooked?”

…then treat that as Green / Amber / Red.

## 8. Always-On Lever #1 — Build the Cave

This runs every night, regardless of HR.

### 8.1 Light — One Warm Lamp, Kill the Glare

Even moderate light during sleep (around 100 lux, like an average lit room) raises night-time HR, reduces HRV, and worsens next-morning insulin sensitivity compared to very dim light. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35286195/) )

So:

- In the bedroom at night:  
    - One **small warm light** (lamp, warm LED, or incandescent light).  
    - Avoid bright overhead lighting, especially cool/blue.  
- If you need a night light:  
    - Keep it low and warm (amber/orange).

Rule:

> **Lamp on = day closing.**\
> **Lamp off = we’re done.**

Screens:

- Try to avoid at the end.  
- If you must:  
    - Lowest brightness, warm mode on, short interactions.

### 8.2 Temperature — Cold Cave, Warm You

Sleep onset works best when:

- Core body temperature can drop  
- Skin (especially hands/feet) is warm enough to dump heat

A warm bath/shower 1–2 hours before bed improves sleep onset and efficiency by speeding that cooling phase. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31102877/) )

So:

- Room: cool enough that **without** a blanket you’d feel a bit cold.  
- With blanket: comfortably cozy.  
- In Singapore, that usually means ***  
    - AC on; fan is fine too.  
    - Avoid hot, stuffy rooms.

Too hot = HR stays higher, more wake-ups. Too cold and drafty = shivering → also higher HR. We’re aiming for “thermally boring.”

### 8.3 Cocoon — One Comfortable Blanket

Pick one main blanket:

- Breathable  
- Comfortable weight (light–medium is fine; heavy is optional)  
- Lives on the bed and **only** on the bed

We want your nervous system to associate:

> “This blanket = end of day.”

**Heat nuance (especially in SG):**

- If you run hot or sweat easily:  
    - Prioritise **breathability** over weight.  
    - Cotton/linen is your friend; synthetics can be a swamp.

**Breathing nuance:**

- If you have asthma, COPD, or sleep apnea:  
    - Be careful with very heavy / weighted blankets; comfort and easy breathing beat pressure.

## 9. Warm Shower Lever

This is the simplest physiological lever.

- Water: warm, not scalding  
- Time: ~10–15 minutes  
- Timing: ideally **1–2 hours before bed**

Evidence: a 2019 meta-analysis of warm baths/showers 40–42.5 °C 1–2 hours pre-bed found shorter sleep onset and better sleep efficiency. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31102877/) )

Mechanism: skin blood vessels open → you dump heat afterward → core temp drops → body reads “night mode.”

If life is hectic, even 5–8 minutes helps.

## 10. Magnesium Lever (Training Wheels, Not Forever)

### 10.1 Why Use It

Magnesium:

- Is involved in nervous system calming  
- May reduce stress hormones and improve subjective sleep quality in people with poor sleep  
- Is cheap and low risk in _healthy_ adults

Recent RCTs with magnesium bisglycinate show improvements in sleep quality and insomnia scores vs placebo in poor sleepers. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alicegwalton/2018/01/14/writing-before-bed-may-help-you-fall-asleep-faster/) )

### 10.2 How to Use It

- Form: glycinate / bisglycinate / L-threonate  
- Dose: ~200–300 mg **elemental** magnesium  
- Timing: 60–90 minutes before bed, ideally with a bit of food

Use during:

- Rough periods  
- Amber/Red weeks  
- While you’re stabilising sleep habits

### 10.3 Safety Caveats

Talk to a doctor first if you:

- Have **kidney disease / dialysis**  
- Take meds that interact with magnesium (certain antibiotics, muscle relaxants, some heart meds)  
- Have significant heart rhythm issues

Magnesium is renally cleared; in those cases it’s not a casual supplement.

### 10.4 Long-Term Goal

You should be able to **sleep decently without it** once cave + routine + cognitive resets are dialled in.

## 11. Creatine Lever (Morning Armour)

### 11.1 What It’s For

Creatine monohydrate:

- Buffers brain and muscle energy  
- Seems especially helpful under **metabolic stress**, including sleep deprivation

A 2024 trial using a single high dose (~0.35 g/kg) during 21 h of sleep deprivation found better cognitive performance and favourable changes in brain high-energy phosphate levels vs placebo. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9) )

Other reviews note that creatine can improve some aspects of cognition under stress, but overall evidence is mixed and not strong enough to claim a guaranteed effect for everyone. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11574456/) )

### 11.2 How We Use It in Level 1

**Baseline use (optional, conservative):**

- 3–5 g creatine monohydrate once a day  
- With breakfast or first proper meal  
- Most healthy adults tolerate this well

Think of it as:

> “Background brain armour,”\
> Not “sleep replacement.”

**Rescue dose (heavily caveated):**

- Very rarely, if:  
    - You had **<4 hours** sleep  
    - You have a demanding day  
    - You are generally healthy and already tolerate creatine well

…you _could_ use a one-off higher dose in the 10–15 g range, split across the morning. That moves toward the research dosing while:

- Reducing GI upset vs a single 20+ g bolus  
- Staying in “still within what athletes commonly do” land

**We explicitly do **_**not**_** encourage** routine 20–25 g one-shot “rescue” doses as a normal habit.

### 11.3 Hard Lines

- If you have **kidney disease**, get medical advice first.  
- Creatine is **never** a license to chronically sleep 4–5 hours a night. It only helps your brain limp through _occasional_ bad nights.

## 12. Cognitive Reset Levers (The Inside of the Hack)

You don’t need all six SPL Cognitive Reset pathways for sleep.\
Level 1 uses **four**:

1. **Breathwork Reset** – for a wired body  
1. **Brain Dump Reset** – for spinning thoughts  
1. **Somatic Discharge Reset** – for a braced body  
1. **Creative Output Reset** – for emotional sludge

We **avoid** close-to-bed:

- Pattern Interrupt (puzzles, games → stimulating)  
- Heavy, high-stakes Coregulation (deep emotional talks at 11:30 pm)

### 12.1 Breathwork (Default Sleep Lever)

- Pattern: inhale 4s → exhale 6s  
- Through the nose, relaxed  
- Duration: 5–15 minutes lying or sitting

Slow breathing in this range improves vagal tone and can reduce HR and sleep disturbance in small trials. ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/shower-before-bed) )

**What “success” looks like:**

- You feel physically softer, heavier, slower  
- HR often drifts down a bit  
- Thoughts get less sticky

### 12.2 Brain Dump (To-Do List Journaling)

5–10 minutes with paper and pen:

- Write a **specific** to-do list for tomorrow/the week.  
- Tasks, errands, “don’t forget” items.  
- Close notebook. That’s where “tomorrow” lives now.

In a lab study, people who wrote to-do lists before bed fell asleep significantly faster than those who wrote about completed tasks; more detailed lists → faster sleep. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/) )

We’re using that as a targeted “cognitive offload.”

### 12.3 Somatic Discharge (3-Pose Sequence)

When you feel physically braced or hunched:

1. Child’s pose – 1–2 minutes  
1. Forward fold (standing or seated) – 1–2 minutes  
1. Supine twist each side – 1–2 minutes

No hero stretches. Just long, easy holds and breathing.

Body-based relaxation (including PMR and similar) improves sleep quality and reduces fatigue across multiple clinical groups. Mechanism: less sympathetic drive, more parasympathetic tone. ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/shower-before-bed) )

### 12.4 Creative Output (Low-Stakes Expression)

For foggy / heavy mood, but not sharp panic:

- 5–15 minutes of:  
    - Doodling  
    - Colouring  
    - Writing 10 nonsense lines  
    - Sketching garbage

Goal: externalise internal noise, not “make art.”

## 13. The One Hard Habit — Book, Not Phone

This stays as the main behaviour change.

**The One-Book Rule:**

- Last ~30 minutes before intended sleep:  
    - Phone is parked away from your body  
    - You use a **physical book** or e-ink

Evidence: screens and light-emitting e-readers in the late evening delay melatonin, push circadian phase later, and worsen next-morning alertness vs print reading. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35286195/) )

Reality clause:

- You _will_ sometimes scroll.  
- Protocol reaction:  
    - When you notice you’re done scrolling:  
        - Put phone down  
        - Do 2–3 minutes of slow breathing or one somatic pose  
        - Then lights off (or book if still awake)

We grade on **damage reduction**, not perfection.

## 14. Zone Playbooks (Behaviour-First, HR Optional)

Remember: zones can come from **HR**, or just from **“how cooked do I feel?”**

### 14.1 Green – “Soft Landing”

You feel reasonably calm; HR, if measured, is close to floor (Δ ≈ 0–5).

**Intent:** Maintain the groove.

**Typical stack:**

1. Cave on (light, cool, blanket).  
1. Warm shower if convenient.  
1. One light Cognitive Reset:

    - 5–10 min breathwork **or**  
    - 5–10 min brain dump **or**  
    - Short somatic 3-pose **or**  
    - 5–10 min doodle.

1. Magnesium: optional.  
1. One-Book Rule (soft).

You **may** notice HR drift down a couple beats during the reset, but we don’t chase it.

### 14.2 Amber – “Use the Toolkit Properly”

You feel wired/tight; HR, if measured, 6–12 bpm above floor.

**Intent:** Help the system let go of some load.

**Stack:**

1. Cave tightened:

    - Kill overhead lights  
    - Make sure room is actually cool

1. Warm shower: move it up the priority list.  
1. One deliberate Cognitive Reset (pick what’s loudest: body / thoughts / mood).  
1. Magnesium: consider adding if recent nights were rough and you’re medically safe.  
1. Creatine: keep baseline 3–5 g in the morning.  
1. Book-not-phone as best as you can; if you scroll, do a mini-reset after.

If you like data:

- You _can_ glance at HR before/after the reset as a curiosity:  
    - If it drops → nice.  
    - If it doesn’t but you feel calmer → still a win.  
    - If neither → you still practised; drop the number and move on.

### 14.3 Red – “Do Not Dig Deeper”

You feel absolutely smoked or sick; HR, if measured, ≥13 bpm above floor.

**Intent:** Damage control only.

**Stack:**

1. Cave non-negotiable:

    - As dark as life allows  
    - Actually cool room  
    - Familiar blanket

1. Warm shower: default unless truly impossible / medically contraindicated.  
1. Cognitive Reset:

    - Default: 8–15 min breathwork  
    - If thoughts are catastrophic: 5–10 min brain dump → then breathwork

1. Magnesium: strongly recommended (if kidneys/meds allow).  
1. Creatine:

    - Keep baseline 3–5 g  
    - _Very rarely_ consider a split higher dose next morning if you’re otherwise healthy and the day is brutal.

1. One-Book Rule if you can; if not, treat phone as anaesthetic not crime, and always finish with a tiny reset.

**Plus:**\
Trigger your **weekly alarmless morning** as soon as practical (see next).

We do **not** try to “win” Red nights. We just stop them digging a deeper hole.

## 15. Weekly Alarmless Morning — Forgiveness Engine

Once a week:

- No alarm if life allows  
- No early brunch or call if you can avoid it  
- Sleep until your body wakes on its own

This is your **built-in catch-up**.

What the science says:

- Consistently getting ~7–8 hours/night is optimal for health. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7200717/) )  
- For people who are short on weekdays, **modest** (≈1–2 h) weekend catch-up sleep is associated with **lower metabolic syndrome risk and better health markers** than staying short every day. ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7200717/) )  
- But chronic restriction plus big weekend overshoots (huge irregularity) is linked with worse metabolic and cardiovascular outcomes. ([OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/47/11/zsae159/7712720) )

So the SPL stance:

> Weekend catch-up is like bailing water out of a leaky boat.\
> It slows the sinking; it does **not** fix the hole.

Use alarmless mornings as **damage control**, not a permanent excuse to live on 4–5 hours.

## 16. KPI Philosophy (What “Success” Looks Like)

KPIs are **behavioural and compassionate**, not numerical and punitive.

Good KPIs:

1. **Awareness**

    - Most nights, you:  
        - Notice how loaded you feel (Green/Amber/Red, with or without HR)

1. **Lever Pull**

    - On Amber/Red nights, you add **at least one** helpful lever beyond your old default.

1. **Cave Consistency**

    - Light, temperature, and blanket set-up are “just how you do nights” most of the time.

1. **Forgiveness**

    - When you miss pieces:  
        - You do _not_ use it as evidence that you’re broken.  
        - You course-correct gently the next chance you get.

Bad KPIs (we explicitly reject):

- “Did I drop my HR by 5 bpm tonight?”  
- “Did I get 8 hours _every single night_?”  
- “How many nights of perfect phone abstinence did I have?”

We care about **direction**, not perfection.

## 17. Evidence Snapshot (Why This Isn’t Just Vibes)

Very short summary of why the pillars make sense:

- **Warm shower / bath 1–2 h pre-bed**  
    - Systematic review/meta-analysis: improves sleep onset and efficiency when done in that window. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31102877/) )  
- **Light and cave building**  
    - One night of moderate light (100 lux) during sleep vs dim (<3 lux) raises HR, reduces HRV, and worsens insulin sensitivity. Darker, warmer-toned evenings are not aesthetic; they’re metabolic. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35286195/) )  
- **Brain dump / bedtime to-do list**  
    - A 5-minute to-do list before bed led to significantly faster sleep onset vs writing about completed tasks; more detailed lists → quicker sleep. ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29058942/) )  
- **Breathwork & somatic relaxation**  
    - Slow breathing and relaxation reduce sympathetic activity, improve HRV, and improve sleep quality in people with insomnia and in stressed populations. ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/shower-before-bed) )  
- **Creatine and sleep deprivation**  
    - Single high dose creatine during a night of sleep deprivation improved cognitive performance and buffered brain energy changes vs placebo, but EFSA notes overall evidence for general cognitive enhancement is still inconclusive. We treat it as a **supportive tool for the occasional bad night**, not a magic fix. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54249-9) )  
- **Weekend catch-up sleep**  
    - Moderate weekend catch-up (≈1–2 h) in short sleepers is associated with lower metabolic syndrome risk; very large or irregular catch-up patterns are not benign and can relate to higher mortality or poorer cardiometabolic profiles. ([SpringerLink](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41105-023-00460-6) )

Taken together, the SPL Sleep Protocol Level 1 is:

- **Scientifically plausible**  
- **Practical for urban, non-athlete humans**  
- **Designed not to turn sleep into a neurotic hobby**

The core moves are simple:

> Build a cave.\
> Nudge your system down a notch before bed.\
> Read a book instead of doom.\
> Give yourself one alarmless morning.\
> Forgive the rest.
