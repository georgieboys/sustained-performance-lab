A minimal cold-exposure tool for autonomic reset and cognitive clarity, using only a basin of cold water.

## 1. OVERVIEW

In SPL, **face dunks** are the **lightest-weight cold modality** in the system.

The face dunk is the **everyday cold “button”** for the brain, while ice baths are the **occasional high-voltage switch** reserved for when they’re truly needed.

We define a face dunk as:

- Immersing the **face only** (forehead, eyes, cheeks) in **cold water**  
- For **5–15 seconds per dunk**,  
- Repeated **2–3 times**, with relaxed breathing between dunks.

Face dunks sit **below** ice baths and full-body cold in the ladder:

- Base cold: shower cold finish / water-on-head  
- Low-dose cold: **face dunk**  
- Higher-dose cold: ice bath / full-body CWI (advanced)

They are:

- Extremely **accessible** – basin, bucket, or sink.  
- Extremely **time-efficient** – under 5 minutes total.  
- Biologically **loud** for the nervous system, even though the exposure is small.

## 2. PURPOSE

The Face Dunk Block is designed to:

- Provide a **fast, high-signal autonomic reset**\
– flip the system toward parasympathetic (“rest–digest”) via the diving reflex.  
- Act as a **micro cold-plunge for the brain**, not the muscles\
– big impact on state, minimal disruption to training adaptation.  
- Serve as the **default cold tool** when:  
    - Space is limited,  
    - Full-body cold is impractical or contraindicated,  
    - You want a reset without a full plunge.  
- Bridge into heavier cold work\
– a stepping stone before considering full-body immersion for those who qualify.

In SPL, face dunks are the **day-to-day cold lever** for mental state and cognitive clarity.

## 3. MECHANISM & EFFECTS

### 3.1 Diving Reflex / Trigeminocardiac Reflex

Cold water on the face hits receptors of the **trigeminal nerve** and triggers the classic **diving reflex**:

- **↑ Vagal activity** (parasympathetic)  
- **↓ Heart rate** (bradycardia)  
- Shift in autonomic balance toward **“rest–digest”**

This is why cold-face tests are used in labs and clinics to probe vagal and brainstem function: the signal is reliable and strong.

### 3.2 Autonomic and Mood Effects

Short cold-face exposures can:

- Increase **heart rate variability (HRV)** markers of vagal tone.  
- Blunt aspects of the stress response (e.g. reduced cortisol output in some protocols).  
- Produce a subjective **“jolt + calm”** pattern:  
    - Brief, controlled discomfort  
    - Followed by a clear settling and often improved clarity or mood.

For SPL purposes, this makes face dunks a **high-yield tool for cognitive reset, anxiety spikes, and pre-focus rituals.**

## 4. HOW MUCH “FULL COLD PLUNGE” DO YOU GET?

Face dunk vs full-body cold immersion:

### 4.1 What You Do Get (High Overlap)

Face dunk captures a large chunk of the **autonomic and state-change** benefits of full-body cold:

- Strong **parasympathetic activation** via the diving reflex.  
- Clear **state shift** from frazzled → calmer, more grounded.  
- Rapid, reliable **cognitive reset**.

Working SPL model (conceptual, not lab percentages):

- For **brain / nervous-system / mood effects**, a well-executed face dunk probably gives you on the order of **60–80% of the useful “plunge” signal**\
at perhaps **5–10% of the logistical cost and risk.**

### 4.2 What You Do Not Get (Low Overlap)

Where face dunk is **not** equivalent to a full cold plunge:

- **Core temperature & metabolic effects**  
    - Almost no meaningful core cooling.  
    - Minimal impact on systemic thermoregulatory and metabolic adaptations that are hypothesised with repeated full-body cold.  
- **Muscle / joint recovery**  
    - Local cooling of big muscle groups and joints does not happen.  
    - Very little of the anti-inflammatory / edema-control effect relevant to DOMS and post-event recovery.  
- **Cardiovascular “mechanical” load**  
    - Full-body CWI involves larger shifts in preload/afterload, peripheral resistance, and central circulation.  
    - Face dunk mainly shifts autonomic tone, not full-circulation mechanics.

SPL summary:

- For **nervous system and mood**, face dunk ≈ “mini-plunge with most of what we care about”.  
- For **systemic metabolic, recovery, and muscular adaptation**, it’s **closer to 10–20% of the effect** of full-body cold – useful, but not a substitute.

## 5. FACE DUNK — SPL STANDARD PROTOCOL

### 5.1 Setup

- Container: basin, bucket, or sink deep enough to submerge face to the cheekbones.  
- Water temperature:  
    - Aim for **cold to the touch**; ≤15 °C is ideal if you have ice.  
    - If environment is hot (e.g. Singapore), “cold tap + a bit of ice” is usually enough.

> **Scientific Note: **\
> The diving reflex is potentiated by colder temperatures. While 15°C works, the magnitude of the bradycardia (heart rate slowing) is often temperature-dependent. Water at 0–10°C will produce a sharper, faster "break" in anxiety than water at 15°C, though 15°C is perfectly adequate for daily maintenance.

### 5.2 Execution

- Position:  
    - Stand or kneel with both hands on the basin edge.  
    - You can choose:  
        - Nose and mouth above water (forehead and eyes only), or  
        - Full face under water if you’re comfortable and breathing is calm.

### Dose (per session):

    - Dunk **5–15 seconds**.  
    - Come up, breathe calmly for **30–60 seconds**.  
    - Repeat **2–3 rounds**.  
- Breathing:  
    - No long forced breath holds.  
    - Normal inhale, relaxed longer exhale once you come up.  
    - If you feel panicky or can’t normalise breathing after a dunk, shorten duration or stop.

### 5.3 Placement in SPL

Typical use-cases:

- **Pre-Gateway wake-up** on groggy days.  
- **Midday cognitive reset** instead of a doomscroll break.  
- **Acute anxiety grounding** – a clean, body-first lever.  
- As the **default daily cold practice**, with ice baths reserved for specific, less frequent sessions.

## 6. SAFETY

Face dunks are **safer than full-body CWI**, but not entirely trivial.

Potential concerns:

- Strong **bradycardia** (HR drop) and vagal surge, particularly in some middle-aged or cardiovascular-risk individuals.  
- Theoretical risk of **“autonomic conflict”** (simultaneous strong sympathetic and parasympathetic activation) in vulnerable hearts.

SPL safety rules:

- People with:  
    - Known heart disease or arrhythmias,  
    - Unexplained fainting,  
    - Significant autonomic disorders\
should get **medical advice** before doing frequent, strong cold-face work.  
- No:  
    - Breath-hold games,  
    - Competitive “who can stay under longest?” challenges.  
- Start with:  
    - Cooler (not extreme) water,  
    - Shorter dunks,  
    - Respect for body feedback.

You should finish a face-dunk block feeling:

- Awake, clearer, and calmer,  
- Not dizzy, nauseous, or “off”.
