# Adjunct Modalities

Optional toys for a mandatory chassis.

# 1. OVERVIEW

Adjunct modalities are **everything that helps the system** but **is not required** for the system to work.

The SPL Canon is built on a hard rule:\
If you need a special room, a subscription, a gadget, or a big pile of money to do it, it cannot be Canon.

Adjuncts live **around** the Canon:

- They can accelerate progress.  
- They can make compliance easier.  
- They can sharpen specific levers (sleep, stress, pain, mood).  
- They can add a sense of ritual and “feel good” to the process.

But the Canon must stay **universally executable**: bodyweight, basic tools, a bit of floor, a place to hang from, cheap kettlebell(s), paper and pen. So adjuncts are clearly labelled as **optional**, with two main filters:

1. **Friction & Cost** – how much money, space, equipment, admin, or hassle is required?  
1. **Evidence & Effect Size** – how strong is the science, and how big is the actual effect?

Adjuncts are what we plug in **after** those questions are answered.

---

# 2. WHY ADJUNCTS LIVE OUTSIDE THE CANON

## 2.1 The Canon’s design constraints

The Canon has simple design laws:

- It must be **globally reproducible** with minimal equipment.  
- It must work for **normal humans** with jobs, families, and limited time.  
- It must not require **lab toys, boutique studios, or medical-grade hardware**.

That means:

- Dead hangs stay, but inversion tables are adjunct.  
- Kettlebell swings stay, but fancy force plates are adjunct.  
- Brain Dump stays, but $2,000 neurofeedback headsets are adjunct.

Adjuncts are not “lesser”. They just **violate at least one of the Canon constraints**: cost, access, complexity, or universality.

## 2.2 Cost and access: the first hard line

Some modalities have very solid evidence but terrible accessibility:

- Sauna (if you don’t already have access).  
- Ice baths / plunge pools.  
- Float tanks, zero-gravity rooms, high-end sensory spaces.  
- Clinical neurofeedback.  
- Structured HRV labs, polysomnography, etc.

From an SPL perspective:

- **They can be great.**  
- **They cannot be mandatory.**

You do not fail SPL because you don’t have a sauna, plunge, or a high-end headset. The system is deliberately built so that the **80–90% of benefit comes from the Canon**, and adjuncts are clearly marked “bonus, not baseline”.

## 2.3 Evidence vs friction

Some tools are:

- **High evidence + low friction** → candidates for Canon if cost stays low.  
- **High evidence + high friction** → stay adjunct.  
- **Low evidence + low risk + high subjective value** → “vibes-allowed” adjuncts.  
- **Low evidence + high cost/risk** → not in the system.

Adjuncts live in that middle band: “good enough to be useful, not universal enough to be mandatory”.

# 3. HOW SPL ORGANISES ADJUNCT MODALITIES

Within SPL, adjunct modalities are grouped by **what they primarily target**, not by how trendy they are.

Examples of categories:

- **Neuroacoustic & sensory**\
Binaural beats, soundscapes, visual fields, sensory-reduction or sensory-shaping spaces (dark rooms, tatami rooms, zero-gravity loungers).  
- **Autonomic & respiratory tools (beyond Canon basics)**\
Canon already uses Breathwork Reset. Adjuncts here are gadgets and environments layered on top: respiratory trainers, HRV biofeedback devices, specialised breathing apps / devices.  
- **Neuroregulation tech**\
Neurofeedback, certain stimulation devices, specialised wearables that claim to modulate arousal or sleep via tactile/vibratory/other cues.  
- **Somatic & manual adjuncts**\
Massage, bodywork, myofascial tools, certain forms of hands-on work that help reduce pain or improve compliance with movement blocks.  
- **Thermal & environmental**\
Saunas, steam rooms, cold plunges, contrast therapy, face dunks, controlled heat rooms, recovery pools.  
- **Monitoring & insight tools**\
Wearables, continuous glucose monitors (CGMs), sleep trackers, HRV monitors that **don’t fix anything by themselves**, but can change behaviour if used well.

Each adjunct is plugged into the system with three questions:

1. **What system are we targeting?** (nervous, metabolic, musculoskeletal, behavioural)  
1. **Where does it sit relative to the Gateway Protocol?** (before, after, or independent)  
1. **What’s the minimum dose where it actually matters?**

# 4. THE SPL EVIDENCE / “VIBES” LADDER

**Note: We are still finalising this grading system**

Adjunct modalities get graded on two axes:

- **Evidence Grade** – how strong and consistent is the science?  
- **Vibes Grade** – how much of the effect is likely “this feels nice and meaningful to me” vs clearly measurable physiology?

We don’t pretend the vibes don’t matter; we just **label them** instead of smuggling them in as “biohacking”.

## 4.1 Evidence Grade

A simple working ladder** ****(Under Development)** :

- **Grade A – Strong evidence, clear signal**\
Multiple decent-quality studies, plausible mechanisms, effect sizes that are not trivial, and a dose range that’s reasonably known.\
SPL stance: “We like this. Still optional if it fails the cost/access test.”  
- **Grade B – Mechanistically solid, emerging / mixed data**\
Plausible mechanisms, some supportive data, but mixed results, small samples, or confounds.\
SPL stance: “Promising, allowed, but treated as experiment, not doctrine.”  
- **Grade C – Low evidence, anecdote-heavy**\
Mechanisms may be speculative, data weak or absent, most of the case built on user reports.\
SPL stance: “This is vibes territory. You may still use it if cost and risk are low, but we don’t pretend it’s a core driver.”  
- **Grade D – Speculative / contested, or risk > benefit**\
Either the evidence is negative, or the risk profile is ugly, or the mechanism contradicts established biology.\
SPL stance: “Not part of SPL. If people do it anyway, it’s outside the Lab.”

Each adjunct in the system will eventually carry a clear label like:\
**Evidence Grade: A (cardiometabolic)** or **Evidence Grade: C (subjective relaxation)**.

## 4.2 Vibes Grade

The second axis is less formal but still real: **how much of the benefit is ritual, identity, and subjective experience?**

Some things are:

- **Evidence-high, vibes-low**\
Not very “sexy”, but they work (e.g. boring breathing drills, consistent sleep schedule, basic walks in daylight).  
- **Evidence-high, vibes-high**\
Saunas, certain forms of immersive spaces, structured group rituals. People both “feel” them and the data has teeth.  
- **Evidence-low, vibes-high**\
A particular tea before bed, a specific scent in the tatami room, favourite lo-fi playlist. These can still matter for adherence and emotional state, as long as we **don’t lie about what we know**.

SPL’s stance:

- Vibes are allowed.  
- Vibes are never enough **by themselves** to upgrade something into the Canon.  
- Whenever we lean heavily on vibes, we say so.

# 5. WHY SOME “GOOD” MODALITIES STAY ADJUNCT

There are tools that are **scientifically respectable** but still remain outside the Canon because they break our universality rules.

Examples of why:

- **Too much money**\
Building or renting sauna space, ice baths, high-end neurofeedback, float tanks, elaborate AR/VR setups. Fantastic if you have them, but they immediately create an economic barrier.  
- **Too much infrastructure**\
Need for dedicated rooms, plumbing, soundproofing, regulation, ongoing maintenance. That’s fine for a Lab or clinic; it is not fine as a prerequisite for health.  
- **Too much dependence on a provider**\
Tools that only work if a specialist is present (certain clinical therapies, some forms of manual work, med-grade tech). Again: helpful, but not universally accessible.

SPL is anti-fragile by **designing the core to assume you have almost nothing**. Adjuncts then become **bonus levers** for those who have the resources and interest.

# 6. HOW TO USE ADJUNCTS INSIDE SPL

Adjuncts should never be used as an excuse to **skip the Canon**.

The order is:

1. **Gateway Protocol and Canon first.**\
Get the basics of movement, strength, VO₂, and cognitive hygiene online.  
1. **Then ask: what is still noisy?**\
Is it sleep? Pain? Stress reactivity? Recovery after sessions? Adherence? Mood?  
1. **Choose adjuncts for specific jobs.**

    - Thermal work to support recovery, mood, and cardiovascular health.  
    - Neuroacoustic tools to help down-shift or focus before sessions.  
    - Somatic/soft-tissue work to increase comfort and allow better participation in the Canon.  
    - Monitoring tools to tighten feedback loops on sleep, stress, or glucose.

1. **Treat every adjunct as an experiment.**

    - Define a hypothesis (“Sauna 3×/week will improve my sleep and soreness scores”).  
    - Run it for long enough to matter.  
    - Track a small number of metrics.  
    - Keep or discard based on outcomes, not marketing.

Adjuncts are **scalpels**, not religion. They solve specific problems, for specific people, at specific times.

---

# 7. SAFETY, BOUNDARIES, AND DISCLOSURE

A few non-negotiables:

- **Medical context matters.**\
Thermal work, cold exposure, certain neuromodulation tools, and some breathing patterns are not trivial if you have cardiovascular, respiratory, neurological, or metabolic conditions. Medical clearance is not performative; it’s practical.  
- **Risk vs reward is always local.**\
What’s low risk for one person (mild sauna, short cold dips) can be high risk for another. SPL gives frameworks, not direct prescriptions.  
- **Adjuncts must not replace fundamentals.**\
No amount of ice, infrared light, or binaural beats will compensate for chronic sleep deprivation, ultra-processed diets, or zero strength work.

So the default stance is:

> The Canon is the chassis. Adjunct modalities are optional attachments.\
> Enjoy them, experiment with them, but don’t confuse them with the vehicle itself.

---

# 8. THE POINT OF ALL THIS

The whole reason to formalise “adjunct modalities” is to prevent two traps:

1. **The Gadget Trap** – where health becomes a collection of expensive toys, and people without those toys feel locked out.  
1. **The Purist Trap** – where anything enjoyable or subjective is dismissed because it doesn’t fit into a narrow evidence box.

SPL takes a third path:

- Build a **hard, simple, evidence-anchored Canon** that almost anyone can run.  
- Surround it with **clearly labelled adjuncts** that range from lab-solid to “vibes with guardrails”.  
- Stay honest about what moves the needle, what’s optional, and what’s just pleasant ritual.

Adjunct modalities, done this way, turn the Lab from a bare toolkit into a richer ecosystem—**without ever putting the toys in charge.**
